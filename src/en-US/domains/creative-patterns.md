## 8. Use auxiliary patterns when needed

Generalize only the use and type of layer. Put concrete content in the target-specific dynamic layer and do not promote it into fixed evaluation criteria.

- **Transforming into experience:** Distribute meaning across four paths—body and sensation; cognition; action and relationship; environment and composition. For harm suffered by real people, first consider representing it structurally. In short forms, integrate the paths into one expression.
- **Integration into existing functions:** Before adding a new element, determine whether an additional role can be integrated into existing prose, action, conversation, function, or procedure.
- **Repetition and explicitness:** Change the relationship or function on each repetition. Control explicitness in three levels: latent, suggestive, and explicit. In humor, satire, and advertising, choose the level according to purpose. Treat the recipient's capacity to receive as the upper limit; exposure and removal of escape routes are distinct effects.
- **Temporal structure—the quality of time:** Work with accumulation and recurrence, timing, and given conditions versus tasks. Implement structures corresponding to necessity, fate, or fortune through signs, repetition, coincidence, and cyclical change. Keep correspondences between given conditions and tasks in design materials. Redesign temporal structure when characters or actors lose room for choice.
- **Deep layers:** Distinguish unarticulated bodily responses in an individual; shared experience accumulated in places or objects; and isomorphic structures repeated between local and whole. Use a different framework for each layer and integrate them through a shared principle of change. Show the highest layer once and at low explicitness.
- **Humor:** Distinguish incongruity, superiority, and relief. Examine the ethical consequences of each. When designing humor that does not judge people, rely mainly on mechanisms other than superiority. Design expectation formation and connect it to the result. Do not explain the effect in the final artifact; communicate through structure.
- **Emotion:** Classify emotions by generation mechanism, effect, example, and placement rather than by emotion names alone. Consider how an earlier emotion changes the intensity and interpretation of later emotions.
- **Series and multiple works:** Assume recipient memory decays and reintroduce necessary information. Repetition that seems excessive in a standalone work may function as reintroduction in a series. Give each installment an independent entry route and distribute accumulation across multiple paths. When several works handle the same structure, assign a primary function to each work or layer.
- **Re-description through another field's vocabulary:** Create different entry routes for different recipients. Choose vocabulary by structural correspondence. Re-description adds a path of understanding to a claim supported by independent grounds.
