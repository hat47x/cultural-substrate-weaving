## 6. Check character consistency

Use Taiheki not as an empirical theory, but as an external typology for checking consistency in character portrayal. Limit it to consistency checking; do not use it for diagnosis or personality evaluation.

Taiheki has three functions here.

1. It introduces a perspective separate from the author's own classifications. Self-made checklists tend to emphasize dimensions the author already notices. An external typology can introduce bodily and situational dimensions the author did not select, such as appetite under tension, patterns of fatigue, or center of gravity.
2. It examines body, posture, center of gravity, and response in addition to psychology.
3. It examines the relationship between the first component—voluntary, surface, social, and tension-state—and the second component—involuntary, deep, personal, and relaxed-state.

When another body-centered external typology serves the purpose equally well, choose the typology that fits the objective.

Validate characters being designed, described, or revised using both bodily material and disconfirming information. Use Taiheki for consistency checking and retain it as an internal model.

When material is insufficient, defer the judgment and retain missing information as unresolved. Even with sufficient material, record what the type cannot explain as target-specific information alongside the type.

Determine the type after developing the portrayal. Early determination makes later portrayal follow the type rather than the character. Apply it to characters described in depth and exclude characters who are only mentioned. For real people, stay within observable information and distinguish the model from diagnosis or personality facts.
