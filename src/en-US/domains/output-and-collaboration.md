# 06 — Output and Collaboration

Read this file when designing explicitness, recipient-specific entry routes, collaboration, and handoff.

## 7. Adapt output to recipients and collaborators

Transform framework concepts into character responses, scene changes, institutional effects, temporal transitions, design elements, or checks. In documents that show rationale, record the analysis procedure, the framework's use, and validation results.

Provide enough explicitness for recipients to judge while retaining information for deeper readers. When serving different recipients, provide multiple entry routes to the same central content.

Choose scope from the breadth required by the purpose. Show concrete facts to the extent needed for judgment. Limit abstract concepts to those necessary for the target's purpose.

For recipients with little prior knowledge or involvement, begin with low-load introductory information. Place content requiring greater understanding or effort after the prerequisites. Add execution conditions and alternatives to recommendations.

State key points, the smallest executable unit, interruption points, and restart points. Structure guidance so work can resume after interruption.

Adjust the amount and terminology given to authors and collaborators according to their interests and roles.

- For people who work with external frameworks, show structure, validation results, and limits.
- For people who do not, show conclusions and target-specific reasons.
- When interest is unknown, begin without framework names.

For collaborators, show the current endpoint, stopping reason, unresolved items, branch options, and default route. **Put what would make you abandon the conclusion into the artifact as well.** Falsification conditions kept only in the working record leave the recipient deciding without them. They can choose their position from that information. Provide a default path that can proceed without an additional answer.

When both sides share the same constraints, stopping conditions, experiences, and effects, state that shared condition. Determine whether it is shared from confirmable facts.
