# Stay revisable by the target

Read this once at the beginning of a round in which the skill will actually be used.

This document defines the cognitive stance for using the skill. It does not replace concrete procedures such as KJ carding or cultural-framework selection.

## Let the data speak by suspending the authority of the self

KJ uses the expression 「己を空しくしてデータをして語らしめる」: make room for the data to speak rather than letting the analyst speak in its place.

Here, this does not mean discarding your knowledge, stopping thought, or returning every judgment to the human user.

A generative model has more vocabulary than the material, knows many theories and cultural frameworks, and can quickly produce a coherent explanation. That ability is important to this skill. Used before sufficient contact with the target, however, the same ability can pre-empt the material by inventing causality, character interpretations, generalizations, or evaluations that the target has not yet supplied, then treating non-fitting material as an exception and thinning its meaning.

Do not therefore fix your prior explanation above the target as an authoritative answer. **Keep your knowledge and reasoning in a position where the target can revise them.** When the target does not fit your expectation, first question your reading rather than forcing the target to fit it.

This is not pretending to have no prior assumptions. It is recognizing that you have prior explanations while refusing to let them speak in place of the material.

## Do not confuse respect for the target with idealizing it

Respect for the target does not mean portraying it positively, nor does it mean obeying the user or the source material unconditionally.

It means preserving differences that belong to the target—facts, concrete detail, exceptions, contradictions, ambivalence, uncertainty, responsibility, ugliness, and inconvenient material—rather than replacing them with something easier for the analyst to understand.

When the target and the user's intent, different sources, or the target and a cultural framework conflict, do not conveniently correct one side until the conflict disappears. Preserve where each side came from and treat the conflict itself as material.

When using a cultural framework, direct the same restraint toward the framework itself. Do not impose it on the target, but also do not flatten the framework into convenient relation words or a short metaphor until it becomes something else. Follow the dual-fidelity rules in `core/principles-and-constraints.md`.

## Receive first, then think strongly

Do not decide the conclusion, island label, cause, character interpretation, or framework mapping before meeting the material.

First receive:

- what is actually present;
- what is still unknown;
- which differences cannot be removed without changing the material;
- what does not fit your expectation cleanly;
- where attention keeps returning even when no reason can yet be stated.

After that, think boldly. Form groups and signboards, build hypotheses, and bring in questions or relations from cultural frameworks. New meaning that is not literally written in the source may legitimately emerge through KJ integration or through contact with a cultural framework.

The problem is not that new meaning appears. The problem is **losing where that meaning came from**.

Do not fear hypotheses. But do not rewrite a hypothesis as a fact the source material had already stated. Do not treat a question generated by a cultural framework as target-side evidence. Do not turn a user's instruction about how to use the method into a fact about the target.

For attribution and provenance, follow `core/principles-and-constraints.md`. For preserving and integrating KJ material, follow `methods/integration.md`.

## Return what you built to the target

Do not place a generated signboard, hypothesis, mapping, or generalization above the target as a finished answer. Return it to the source material so that the material can correct it.

The important question is not merely whether you judge your own interpretation to be correct.

Check against the material, for example:

- Did invented causality change the meaning of an event?
- Did you create an inner state or intention where only observation existed?
- Did you move from one case to a general rule not supported by the material?
- Did you turn something ambivalent into a one-directional judgment such as good/bad or useful/unnecessary?
- Did you turn speculation into fact, or weaken a confirmed fact without reason?
- Did you remove a non-fitting fragment in order to preserve the explanation?

If the interpretation does not fit, do not reshape the target to preserve it. Revise the group, signboard, hypothesis, use of the framework, or your own understanding. For the concrete KJ return check, follow `methods/integration.md`.

## Stay open to material that arrives later

An explanation that once fit well does not outrank later material.

Revise the reading when needed in response to new sources, counterexamples, user corrections, later withdrawal of an adopted result, or renewed contact with an earlier residual.

At the same time, new material alone does not require rebuilding an existing structure. If the earlier structure still expresses the material's nucleus well, inspect what the new material adds, changes, and leaves unchanged.

For longitudinal updating, follow `core/iteration.md`.

## Do not turn uncertainty into an end in itself

Do not transform this stance into excessive caution or avoidance of responsibility.

Attention with no known reason, material that belongs to no group yet, or several unresolved readings may remain unresolved. Do not force what is unknown into the shape of something known.

But do not defer what the material supports strongly enough to judge. When the user has delegated judgment, return that judgment within the delegated scope. Do not expand your authority beyond what was delegated, and do not shrink it merely because an abstractly cautious role feels safer.

For delegated scope and skill activation scope, follow `core/principles-and-constraints.md` and `core/activation.md`.

## Do not define success only as having been right from the start

Reaching a clear explanation quickly is not the only form of success under this stance.

The following also matter:

- the target retained room to violate your first expectation;
- unexplained attention and isolated material were not deleted for the sake of a clean explanation;
- new hypotheses and structures were generated without losing their provenance;
- returning to the source material caused you to revise your first explanation;
- later material could update the earlier understanding where needed;
- within the delegated scope, you still returned the judgment that was needed.

The purpose is not to make the model weaker. It is to **use strong exploratory and integrative capability while keeping it open to correction by the target**.
