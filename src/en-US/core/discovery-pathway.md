# The catalytic discovery pathway and illumination

Read this when you need the overall shape of the work, when you run a framework as a generative rule, or when you bring generated candidates down into real use.

This document describes the experiential pathway that sits above the individual procedures. Its movements are representative examples, not a fixed order or mandatory stages. Adoption, stopping, publication, and action follow the decision authority in `core/principles-and-constraints.md`.

## Movements of the pathway

A researcher sometimes encounters an unfamiliar concept in everyday life and uses it as a catalyst that reorganizes how a question looks. The pathway reconstructs that experience as the following movements. Any movement may return to an earlier one. Using only one movement is also legitimate use.

| Movement | What it does |
|---|---|
| Hold | Set out the question, target material, baseline, and what must not be broken (`methods/scope-and-facts.md`) |
| Encounter | Choose a whole-world framework structurally distant from the current segmentation and touch its structural core (`methods/system-selection.md`) |
| Run | Run the framework as a generative rule while keeping the assumption explicit (below) |
| Cross | Cross the results with target material and look at both resonance and pushback (`methods/transformation.md`). Delegate synthesis of numerous or heterogeneous materials through the connection contract in `methods/integration.md` |
| Land | Make candidates concrete step by step: questions, observation or research, small trials, composition, judgments (below) |
| Savor | Receive what actually happened and return it as new material. Keep unadopted candidates with their marks intact (`core/iteration.md`) |

## Exploratory pass for maximizing catalytic yield

When exploratory use proceeds under the hypothesis that cultural frameworks are useful for cognitive operation, maximize **discovery increment from distinct cognitive operations that ordinary analysis would not readily produce** — not framework count, reading volume, or mapping count.

Do not stop exploration at the first plausible correspondence. Preserve the framework as a whole field long enough to traverse several of its own operational directions, then carry the resulting questions, distinctions, relations, transitions, falsifiers, and trial candidates back to the target.

When exploration is delegated and broad discovery increment is desired, use the following pass. It is not a mandatory fixed order, and an operation the framework does not possess must not be silently invented and attributed to it.

1. **Freeze the baseline**
   - Before opening the framework deeply, record the current question, established target-side facts and relations, unresolved material, and current segmentation.
   - Preserve a comparison point for detecting later replacement of the problem by framework vocabulary.
2. **Open the whole field first**
   - Before mapping the target, recover as much as relevant of the framework's own position set, relation graph, adjacency, opposition, cycles, boundaries, transitions, practice, and symbolic neighborhoods.
   - Do not reduce it early to a few meanings that already seem useful for the target.
3. **Change position**
   - Re-view the same target through placement, vacancy, adjacency, center/periphery, layers, and boundaries.
   - Ask not only where something maps, but what disappears or becomes visible from another position.
4. **Move through the field**
   - Run framework-native predecessor/successor, adjacency, opposition, complement, generation/restraint, cycle, return, and irreversibility when those structures exist.
   - Harvest paths, branches, unreachable states, and temporal gaps that pointwise correspondence tends to hide.
5. **Convert practical wisdom into cognitive operations**
   - Turn framework-native guidance about attention, fitting action, and avoidance into candidates for observation, reversible trials, and falsification.
   - Do not substitute framework practice for domain judgment.
6. **Let the target push back**
   - Inspect misfit, resistance, reversal, excess, and places where the framework would separate what the target keeps together.
   - Preserve any third structure produced by the tension as `cross_field_emergent`, distinct from simple correspondence.
7. **Re-segment through a structurally distant second field**
   - When delegation and resources allow, make light contact with a framework of a different structural type to disturb segmentation fixed by the first.
   - Harvest unique questions, relations, transition candidates, and disagreements rather than accumulating confirmations.
8. **De-bind back into target language**
   - Rewrite candidates so they remain usable as target-directed questions, observations, comparisons, or trials without requiring framework names or symbols.
   - Candidates that collapse under de-binding may still be preserved as framework-specific scaffolding with provenance intact.
9. **Integrate candidates as material, then return to the target**
   - Keep questions, distinctions, relation candidates, transition candidates, falsifiers, observation targets, trials, and residuals as separate material; delegate to a compatible `affinity-synthesis` when useful.
   - Do not let framework-derived cards predetermine grouping or labels.
   - Return the resulting candidates to target-side sources and observations, distinguishing what survives independently, what is pushed back, and what remains unresolved.

A framework's available cognitive operations can be summarized like this:

| Framework structure | Question when used as a cognitive operation |
|---|---|
| Positions / types | Where can the current segmentation be split differently? |
| Adjacency / neighborhood | What becomes visible from what lies nearby rather than from direct correspondence? |
| Vacancy | Without declaring a target deficiency, what inquiry does the empty position generate? |
| Opposition / complement | What does the current account hide when viewed from its opposite or complement? |
| Transition / path | What is presupposed, what can happen next, and where might return become impossible? |
| Cycle / phase | How does the same thing look at another time, scale, or phase? |
| Boundary / center-periphery | Where does classification break, and what remains at the boundary? |
| Practical wisdom | Where should attention go next, and what observation or trial could obtain a response from reality? |
| Symbolic neighborhood | What new question can connect things that ordinary vocabulary keeps far apart? |

Do not collapse outputs from these operations immediately into one explanation. When useful, externalize at least `question / distinction / relation-candidate / transition-candidate / falsifier / observation-or-trial / residual` separately. This preserves framework richness long enough for later affinity synthesis to recompose it with target material.

This pass does not make maximum depth or multiple frameworks mandatory. It is an execution form for cases where exploratory use is meant to draw broadly on the cognitive possibilities of cultural frameworks while avoiding premature convergence.

## Run the framework as a provisional generative rule

Treat the framework's rules **provisionally** as generative rules that produce a world, not as summaries induced from observation. Posit "if this system generated the world," then run it without hesitation.

- **Placement**: in which position do the target, its elements, actors, or phases sit?
- **Vacancies**: what should occupy the other positions of the closed set? What does an empty position ask?
- **Transitions**: what came before and what comes next? What cannot return? How do generation and restraint work?
- **Complementary positions**: how does the same thing look from complementary or opposing positions?
- **Practice**: in this state, what is attention said to belong on, and what is it fitting to try?

When the analyst supplies a layer the framework does not have, such as transition rules, and runs it anyway, say so explicitly. Do not remove the assumption clause ("seen as this system") until the candidate has received a response from reality. What the run produces is `framework_generated`; attributing it to the target requires meeting the attribution-use conditions in `methods/framework-application.md` again.

## Illuminate: do not prohibit toxins; keep the marks

Generated material usually becomes harmful not because its content is bold but because it loses its marks of origin, assumption, and reach. A candidate that has lost its marks tends to circulate as fact carrying the framework's authority, as the truth about the whole, or as the target's own voice.

This skill does not stop this with uniform gates. It shifts viewpoint with each movement of the pathway and illuminates candidates.

- **Light of origin**: which position or operation of which framework did this come from — or did it come from the target, or from contact?
- **Light of assumption**: is the generative rule's assumption clause still attached to the candidate?
- **Light of the whole**: seen from complementary positions or transitions of the same framework, what does this candidate hide and what does it harden? When a type is seen as a state within transitions, it becomes a point of passage rather than a fixed essence.
- **Light of the target**: does the target material support this candidate or push back? Is a candidate that fits nowhere still visible as isolated?
- **Light of the concrete**: can you say who would observe what, when, and how, and what would change your mind?

Light brings visibility, not judgment or deletion. A projection or a nearly evaporated assumption that illumination exposes can be kept as information about the framework's bias or as learning for the next piece of work. Adoption returns to the external decision authority.

Do not turn illumination into a form field imposed on every candidate every time. Use it where losing a mark would change later judgment. When useful, a candidate can carry the following lantern.

```text
candidate:
origin: framework:<framework/position/operation> | target:<ref> | contact
as_if: <assumption clause still in force>
whole_view: <what complementary positions or transitions revealed>
reach: question | observation | trial | composition | claim | action
```

`reach` records how close to reality the candidate is being brought. Its value does not grant or deny permission.

## Land: concreteness becomes light

When bringing an insight down into real use, make the candidate concrete step by step.

1. **Question**: turn it into a concrete question directed at the target.
2. **Observation or research**: decide what to look at or look into to get closer to an answer.
3. **Small trial**: turn it into a reversible action that can receive a response from reality.
4. **Composition**: use it as a compositional resource for writing, design, planning, or similar work.
5. **Judgment or action**: move on to adoption, publication, or execution.

This sequence shows increasing impact on reality. How far to go, and which steps to skip, follow the external decision authority and delegation.

Concreteness is itself illumination. Assumptions and projections that stayed hidden inside framework vocabulary come to light once you try to say who, what, and when. Because candidates closer to reality require more concreteness, the amount of light grows with reach naturally, without a uniform threshold.

Points of attention and actions obtained from a framework's practice layer serve as footholds for landing. Do not let them substitute for domain quality standards, safety standards, or professional judgment. Receive domain standards from the caller's context or domain skills.

## Savor: make results the next material

Receive what actually happened, user corrections, and post-adoption withdrawals as new material. When results push back against the framework reading, record which premise of that reading gave way. Do not rewrite the origin of a candidate that was later supported as if it had come from the target. Reopening across multiple rounds follows the connection contract in `core/iteration.md`.
