# Maya calendar systems: Tzolk’in / Haab / Calendar Round / Long Count

## Structural core adopted by CSW

Maya calendrical traditions contain several cycles and time-reckoning systems. CSW does not collapse them into a personality system or divinatory lookup table. The adopted cognitive structure is that **different temporal cycles can run together, so partial recurrence differs from recurrence of the whole configuration**.

The Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian's Living Maya Time presents these core structures:

- Tzolk’in in Yucatec Maya / Chol Q’ij in K’iche’ Maya: a 260-day cycle formed by combining twenty day signs with numbers 1 through 13.
- Haab: a 365-day cycle of eighteen 20-day months plus the five-day Wayeb.
- Calendar Round: the interweaving of Tzolk’in and Haab; the same combination recurs after 18,980 days, or 52 Haab years.
- Long Count: a longer chronological system using units including k’in, uinal, tun, katun, and baktun for events beyond one Calendar Round.

This dossier adopts calendar mechanics as cognitive structure. Personality, fortune, ritual, and day-sign interpretation are separate layers and are not loaded automatically.

## Strong cognitive operations

- coupled-cycle pass: keep two or more cycles distinct and view the current state as a composite coordinate;
- partial/full recurrence: distinguish recurrence on one cycle from recurrence of the full joint state;
- phase-desynchronization: compare points where one cycle aligns while another differs;
- recurrence-distance: ask what returns at which interval rather than treating all repetition as equivalent;
- multi-scale time: hold short cycles, coupled cycles, and long-span coordinates together;
- cycle/count distinction: separate recurrent description from long-span chronological placement.

## Questions returned to the target

- When something appears to repeat, which cycle is actually repeating and what remains different?
- Does the meaning of a local recurrence change when another cycle is out of phase?
- What looks cyclical at a short scale but cumulative or irreversible at a longer scale?
- Has one dominant rhythm hidden another independent phase?
- What multiple target-side conditions would need to align before calling a state a full recurrence?
- If the Maya calendar vocabulary is removed, do the coupled-cycle and recurrence questions still survive in target language?

## Preserve cultural specificity

Do not treat Tzolk’in / Chol Q’ij as merely an abstract 260-day counter, but also do not transport its sacred meanings directly into unrelated targets.

Keep distinct:

- the Yucatec Maya name Tzolk’in and the K’iche’ Maya name Chol Q’ij and their living contexts;
- calendar arithmetic versus day-sign interpretation, ceremony, and day keeping;
- ancient calendrical records versus calendars and ceremonies practiced by Maya communities today;
- position in a calendar cycle versus causal evidence in the target.

Living Maya Time documents contemporary Chol Q’ij new-year ceremonies and day keepers in K’iche’ communities as well as agricultural ceremonies tied to the Haab in Yucatán. CSW therefore does not treat the system only as an extinct ancient code.

## Exploration and attribution

For exploratory use, the framework can generate questions about coupled periods, phase difference, partial recurrence, and long-span temporal coordinates.

Attributing the same cyclic structure to a target requires independently defined target-side periods and observations. A calendar coordinate or day sign is not evidence for causation, personality, or prediction in the target.

## Variant and application boundary

This minimal dossier does not adopt:

- day-sign personality diagnosis;
- generalized fortune telling;
- a single homogenized “Maya spirituality” that erases community differences;
- fixed mappings from Calendar Round or Long Count positions to unrelated modern targets;
- popular 2012 prophecy as a cognitive operation.

If day-sign meanings or ritual practices are used later, add them as a source- and community-specific layer.
