# Llull's late Ars

## Structural core adopted by CSW

Ramon Llull's Ars developed as a system that combines basic terms, figures, letters, definitions, and rules in order to answer questions. The Art changed substantially across its history, so CSW does not treat the early Quaternary and later Ternary phases as one invariant mechanism.

Runtime scope is limited to the later Ternary Art represented by the `Ars generalis ultima` and `Ars brevis`. In this form, the Third Figure represents binary combinations and the rotating Fourth Figure generates ternary combinations. CSW borrows the operation of systematically crossing explicit distinctions so that combinations missed by ordinary association become inspectable questions.

## Strong CSW operations

- combinatorial-enumeration: enumerate binary and ternary combinations systematically rather than by spontaneous recall.
- systematic-crossing: cross different kinds of distinctions to expose relations hidden when each is inspected alone.
- rotation: vary the alignment of the same terms instead of fixing one favored combination.
- unseen-combination-probe: locate combinations never jointly examined in the current material.
- argument-space-expansion: convert combinations into questions, comparisons, possible counterexamples, or research targets rather than conclusions.

## Target return

Ask which distinctions have never been crossed, whether a third dimension reveals a condition hidden in pairs, whether a generated combination is meaningful on the target side, which combinations are impossible under target constraints, and whether existing discussion repeatedly favors only familiar combinations.

## CSW-added constraint

Enumeration grows rapidly. CSW therefore returns generated combinations to target-side constraints and removes impossible, meaningless, or already-examined combinations.

This pruning is a CSW analytic addition, not a native claim about Llull's Art. It does not turn framework-generated combinations into target facts.

## Near-neighbor boundary

Yijing works with a canonical positional configuration and state change. Llull's late Ars systematically crosses selected concepts or distinctions to generate unexamined combinations.

Catuṣkoṭi expands the logical stance around one proposition. Llull crosses multiple independent distinctions in binary or ternary combinations.

The operation is also more than filling a Cartesian table. Historically the Ars linked combinations to definitions, rules, and questions. CSW therefore requires a generated combination to return as a meaningful target question rather than treating formal coverage as success.

## Limits

Do not transfer Llull's theological Dignities or missionary purpose into target-side facts or values. Do not collapse the Quaternary and Ternary phases into one timeless Art.

Combinatorial coverage does not establish validity, completeness, or truth. A combination rejected by target constraints is not a target-side absence.
