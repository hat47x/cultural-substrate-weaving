# Tibetan Buddhist mandala spatial structure

## Structural core adopted by CSW

CSW adopts a deliberately narrow spatial minimum repeatedly documented in Tibetan Buddhist mandalas: center, directional differentiation, gates, nested enclosures, and movement or conceptual approach from outside toward a center.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art and Rubin Museum describe mandalas as diagrams used in Buddhist religious practice and show concrete configurations with central figures, directional differentiation, gateways, enclosing regions, and staged approach. Deity sets, colors, directional correspondences, initiation, and visualization procedures remain lineage- and practice-specific and are excluded from the common runtime core.

## Strong CSW operations

- center-periphery-reframe: ask what kind of centrality is being assumed—spatial, functional, attentional, or institutional.
- directional-role-map: inspect whether sectors or directions differ in role rather than forming one vertical hierarchy.
- nested-boundary-pass: look for multiple enclosures and intermediate regions instead of one inside/outside split.
- gate-threshold-probe: ask whether access must pass through a gate, permission, procedure, or mediating point.
- center-approach-sequence: inspect an outside-to-center path that a static diagram may hide.

## Target return

Ask what makes the center central, whether centrality survives a shifted viewpoint, which intermediate boundaries exist, what mediates entry, whether sectors differ, and whether the spatial questions survive after mandala vocabulary is removed.

## Near-neighbor boundary

Huayan foregrounds whole–part reciprocity and node perspective. Tibetan Buddhist mandala foregrounds explicit spatial organization through center, directions, enclosures, and gates.

Shinto shrine threshold/purification foregrounds participant preparation before a central act. Mandala foregrounds spatial organization and mediated access across multiple boundaries.

## Limits

Do not present Tibetan Buddhist mandalas as one invariant geometry. Do not universalize the colors, deities, directions, or gates of one deity mandala.

Do not simulate initiation, visualization authority, or lineage transmission. Do not treat movement toward a center as inherently superior in the target.
