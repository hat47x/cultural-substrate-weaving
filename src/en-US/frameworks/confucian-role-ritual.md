# Confucian role and li

## Structural core adopted by CSW

In early Confucian material centered on the Analects, li extends beyond formal ceremony to patterned conduct in family, friendship, village, and official relations. Ritual practice is also discussed as part of cultivating dispositions.

CSW does not import historical hierarchy as a norm. It uses relation-conditioned action, the distinction between a role-name and enacted behavior, patterned interaction as context, and repeated practice as a hypothesis about attention or response.

## Strong CSW operations

- role-perspective-switch: reread one event from different relational positions.
- name-behavior-congruence: compare a claimed role or title with observed conduct.
- relation-obligation-map: externalize which relation is carrying which expectation.
- ritual-context-shift: inspect how patterned interaction changes what actions or utterances mean.
- practice-shapes-disposition-probe: turn the traditional cultivation claim into a target-side observation question.

## Target return

Ask which relationship is producing the expectation, whether role labels are supported by conduct, whether legitimate role-demands conflict, and whether an interaction has a different function inside and outside a patterned context.

## Limits

Do not import historical family, gender, political, or status hierarchy as a modern norm. Do not reduce role to personality or li to etiquette. Claims about cultivation do not establish causal effects in an unrelated target.

## Contact selection boundary

Do not activate this framework for an ordinary role-title/authority mismatch that a RACI matrix, approval logs, or access-rights review already exposes.

Consider bounded contact only where **a repeated enacted interaction and participants' relational positions may change the meaning of the same utterance or consultation**, and where ordinary project review has not already surfaced that cognitive job.

Example: a formally valid consultation ends with "any objections?" after the lead has already announced a recommendation. Did participants experience this as an invitation or as a closing formula? This is a **question, not a causal finding**.

Return to target evidence: meeting sequence, participant accounts, actual speech, alternate explanations, before/after or comparison cases, and counterexamples. Do not infer motives from silence or declare a ritualized pattern the cause without evidence.

No-framework is correct if organizational-routines, facilitation, or human-factors analysis has already opened and investigated this question. Remove historical Confucian terms and hierarchy from the target-facing result. Use only provisional, testable questions about enacted interaction and role-positioned expectations.
