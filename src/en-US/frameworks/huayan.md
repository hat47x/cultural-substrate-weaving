# Huayan — whole, part, and mutual inclusion

## Structural core adopted by CSW

CSW uses Chinese Huayan, especially Fazang's whole–part analysis, as a cognitive resource for questioning the assumption that parts are independently fundamental while wholes are merely sums of them.

In Fazang's rafter/building analysis, the whole–part relation is examined through paired aspects including wholeness and particularity, identity and difference, and integration and separateness. A rafter has its role as a rafter through the building in which it functions, while the building also depends on its parts.

CSW does not reduce Huayan mutual inclusion to “everything is the same” or “everything affects everything equally.” The adopted core is that **difference can remain while roles, identities, and relations depend on whole, context, and perspective**.

## Strong cognitive operations

- whole-part reciprocity: ask both how a whole depends on parts and how a part's identity depends on the whole;
- role-defined identity: move an element hypothetically to another whole and inspect which labels or functions cease to hold;
- perspective-through-node: reread the field from one node, then compare what becomes invisible or central from another;
- integration-with-difference: after forming an integrated account, restore differences and resistance that the whole might have erased;
- mutual-dependence probe: ask whether an apparently intrinsic property is sustained by surrounding relations or roles;
- whole-bias / part-bias counter-view: test both over-holistic and over-atomized explanations.

## Questions returned to the target

- Would this part have the same identity outside the present whole?
- Which apparent properties of the part are actually role-dependent?
- Which differences disappeared when the material was integrated into one whole?
- Is something central only from one node's perspective?
- If one part changes, do the identities or roles of other parts change as well?
- If “everything is connected” is removed, which relations are actually supported?
- Do the whole/part, role, and perspective questions survive after Huayan vocabulary is removed?

## Preserve the framework's specificity

- Do not use Indra's Net as a complete summary of Huayan.
- Do not translate mutual dependence into a network model where every edge has equal causal strength.
- Preserve both identity and difference rather than collapsing one into the other.
- Do not assume that decomposition reveals more fundamental independent entities.
- Do not turn Buddhist claims about emptiness or the dharma realm into target-domain facts.
- Do not treat Chinese Huayan, Korean Hwaeom, and Japanese Kegon as one invariant lineage.

## Current Fazang-centered scope

This dossier does not claim to represent every Huayan lineage. It centers the structural arguments for which the current source basis is strongest, especially Fazang:

- rafter/building whole–part analysis;
- interdependence and mutual inclusion;
- holding identity/difference and integration/separateness together;
- metaphors such as the golden lion that challenge the appearance of independently existing things.

Expanding to the Avataṃsaka Sūtra as a whole, Dushun, Zhiyan, Chengguan, Zongmi, Korean Hwaeom, or Japanese Kegon requires additional lineage-specific work.

## Classical-source boundary

CBETA T1881, Notes on the Avataṃsaka Sūtra Golden Lion, contains a root text attributed to Fazang together with later Song commentary by Chengqian. CSW does not flatten the root text and later commentary into one historical layer.

Likewise, later diagrams and modern network metaphors must not be back-projected into Fazang's own terminology.

## Exploration and attribution

For exploratory use, Huayan can generate whole–part reciprocity, role-defined identity, node-perspective, and integration-with-difference questions.

Attributing a Huayan-like structure to a target requires independent target-side evidence. A framework-generated relation does not become a network fact or causal claim merely because the framework exposes it.

## De-binding

Before returning a candidate to the target:

1. remove Huayan, dharma-realm, Indra's Net, and rafter vocabulary unless needed for provenance;
2. restate the candidate in target vocabulary of whole, part, role, context, and perspective;
3. retain which relation was framework-generated;
4. return to target-side source/card material and test whether the relation is independently supported;
5. keep differences that resist integration as residuals;
6. preserve weakening or reframing through target-return audit on the same stable ID.
