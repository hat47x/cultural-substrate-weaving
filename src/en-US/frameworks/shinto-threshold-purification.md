# Shinto shrine threshold and purification

## Structural core adopted by CSW

CSW adopts a bounded structure visible in contemporary shrine entry and pre-ritual purification: marked boundary, approach, preparation, and central action. This is not a claim that all Shinto practice is one shrine-visit protocol.

Jinja Honcho describes the torii as a boundary between shrine sacred space and the ordinary world and places temizu before paying respects. Kokugakuin University references distinguish related but non-identical practices such as temizu, misogi, harae, and shubatsu; shubatsu is performed immediately before rituals.

CSW does not borrow religious efficacy. It borrows the structure in which a boundary changes participation conditions and preparation precedes a central act.

## Strong CSW operations

- threshold-marking: locate an explicit boundary where treatment or state changes.
- before-entry-preparation: separate preparation from the central action.
- path-to-center: distinguish threshold, approach, preparation point, and central interaction.
- state-transition-by-practice: ask what becomes possible, appropriate, or expected after a practice.
- closure-and-return: inspect whether exit, return, or re-entry changes assumptions.

## Target return

Ask where the actual boundary is, what preparation is independently required, whether skipping preparation has observable consequences, whether approach points have different roles, and how the process returns to ordinary state.

## Near-neighbor boundary

Rites-of-passage theory emphasizes separation, liminality, and incorporation across a longer transition. This shrine-entry structure emphasizes a marked entrance and preparation before participation.

Tibetan Buddhist mandala also uses gates and boundaries, but its primary contribution is spatial organization through center, directions, and nested enclosures.

## Limits

Do not transfer sacredness, sin, pollution, or purity into target-side fact or moral worth. Do not treat ritual purification as hygiene or medical efficacy.

Do not present current institutional guidance as invariant across every shrine, region, and historical period. Do not collapse temizu, misogi, harae, shubatsu, and ōharae into one practice.
