# Classical stasis theory

## Structural core adopted by CSW

Greek and Roman rhetorical traditions developed stasis or status theory to locate what a controversy is actually about. Ancient authors and periods do not share one invariant grid, so CSW does not canonize one historical table.

A cross-source minimum useful for CSW distinguishes questions of fact, definition, evaluation or quality, and procedure or competence. The purpose is not four-box classification; it is separating arguments that are answering different questions.

## Strong CSW operations

- dispute-state-separation: split a controversy into factual, definitional, evaluative, and procedural/competence questions.
- fact-before-evaluation-probe: detect evaluation of an event whose occurrence or description is still disputed.
- definition-shift-audit: detect movement from whether something occurred to what it should be called.
- quality-after-concession-probe: expose the evaluative question that remains after the basic event is conceded.
- competence-procedure-probe: separate who, where, when, and by what rule a decision should be made from the substantive dispute.
- stasis-switch-detection: preserve unacknowledged shifts between issue types.

## Target return

Ask what is actually disputed, whether the disagreement survives agreement on fact or definition, which evidence would answer the current issue, and which evidence answers a different issue.

## Variant boundary

Hermagoras, Cicero, Quintilian, Hermogenes, and later traditions organize issues differently. Modern classroom simplifications must not be projected backward as the single ancient system. Multiple stases may coexist.
