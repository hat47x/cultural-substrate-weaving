# Nyāya five-member inference

## Structural core adopted by CSW

Early Nyāya presents a five-member form for inference addressed to others or used in debate: thesis, reason, corroborating example, application to the present case, and conclusion, traditionally pratijñā, hetu, udāharaṇa or dṛṣṭānta, upanaya, and nigamana.

CSW does not adopt five slots as a truth machine. It uses the separation between reason, a more general relation supported by comparison, and the application of that relation to the current target.

## Strong CSW operations

- argument-unfolding: unfold a fluent explanation into thesis, reason, example, application, and conclusion.
- reason-rule-separation: separate the observed reason from the general relation that is supposed to connect reason and conclusion.
- example-counterexample-probe: test whether an example supports the relation rather than merely resembling the case, and look for counterexamples.
- rule-application-audit: inspect whether the claimed relation actually applies to the target.
- inference-gap-detection: preserve missing warrants or applications as residuals.

## Near-neighbor boundary

Aristotle's four causes pluralize kinds of explanation. Catuṣkoṭi reopens proposition space. Jain sevenfold predication conditions assertions by standpoint. Nyāya instead makes the bridge from reason to conclusion inspectable.

## Target return

Ask what the thesis is, what target observation serves as the reason, what independent case supports the proposed connection, why that connection applies here, and which step fails first under a counterexample.

## Limits

Do not present the five-member form as the one invariant structure of all Indian logic. Do not collapse Nyāya, Buddhist logic, Vaiśeṣika, or later Navya-Nyāya. Completing five fields does not make an inference sound, and an illustrative example is not independent evidence by itself.
