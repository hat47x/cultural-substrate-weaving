# Perspective analysis

Use cultural perspectives to reinterpret structures within one target or shared structures and differences across a problem set. This placement differs from material-led affinity grouping: it uses framework distinctions and preserves that origin.

## What to clarify

Explain which relations, conditions, states, changes, boundaries, or scales make the target work or differ for the current purpose. Formal elegance or agreement with a framework does not establish essential structure.

A higher-level perspective means reading the problems through a shared view to compare common and differentiating relations. Higher-level describes the scope of the view, not the framework's truth or authority over the target.

## Align comparison units

Specify the set, each problem, and the actors, situations, and periods compared in target language. Do not silently alternate between an organization, its decision process, and an individual's act. If different scales matter, include their difference in the comparison.

Retain the existing classification and what it struggles to distinguish. When material-led regrouping is needed, delegate to an available affinity-diagramming realization and receive its result as comparison material. Do not prescribe framework positions as affinity group labels.

## Declare the perspective

Check the native partition principle, positions, and relations before mapping to the target. Distinguish classification, temporal phase, relational network, and procedures such as questioning. Do not force every framework into coordinate axes.

Briefly explain which distinction the framework should illuminate, the source and lineage used, the analyst's adaptation to this target, and the concrete information or exceptions the view struggles to handle.

A framework addressing the whole world does not necessarily classify this target exhaustively or exclusively. Allow overlaps, unmapped items, and readings spanning multiple positions.

## Situate qualitatively

Describe what becomes foregrounded when each target is viewed through the perspective. If useful, describe affinity to positions using words such as central, partial, opposed, or unknown. These are attributed judgments, not measurements from a classifier; adapt the vocabulary to the target.

Use a comparable level of explanation for the supporting target material and reasons for competing placements. Do not remove weak correspondence to force a single type. Do not infer personality, destiny, or intention from a position.

Explain closeness or distance relative to the selected view: shared transition conditions, opposing relational directions within one position, or different current states with similar next questions. Numerical distance or probability is unnecessary for such comparison. If requested, define numerical measures and identify the judging subject separately.

## Intersect perspectives

Explain the second perspective by the distinction the first could not make. Distinguish repeating one classification under another framework name from adding a different view.

Here, orthogonality means minimizing conceptual overlap and mutual dependence between perspectives so that their intersection can isolate elements with high independence. Prioritize close approximation to mathematical orthogonality or statistical independence. Different framework names or partition principles alone are insufficient.

Judge orthogonality qualitatively when selecting a combination:

- Is one set of concepts or classifications a paraphrase or derivative of the other?
- Can a position or state in one view vary without determining the position or state in the other, allowing them to vary separately?
- Can the elements isolated by intersection be understood separately and used in comparison or composition? Which dependencies remain?

Treat Wuxing and the five great elements as a highly orthogonal combination of distinct conceptions. This is a reference example of the product's design intent. In use, identify the chosen lineages and partition principles, and read them without imposing one-to-one element correspondence. Shared element names do not establish identical concepts.

Use framework sources, concrete target examples, domain knowledge, and human judgment. Neither numerical scoring nor empirical experiments are prerequisites for selection. Explain where independence appears high and where overlap or dependence remains. Record this qualitative judgment separately from mathematical proof or statistical measurement.

If using a matrix, form each reading separately, then compare the same targets at their intersections. Do not tailor the second placement to agree with the first. Cells need not all be filled. Distinguish target absence, an unread case, out-of-scope material, and insufficient sources.

An intersection can distinguish targets previously treated alike, reveal a shared condition across apparently different targets, expose a conflict requiring new boundaries or comparison units, or retain concrete information neither view handles.

An intersection or two concatenated labels is not itself a third structure. When contact changes a distinction or relation, retain the change and both origins.

## Return to the target and inform judgment

Return the reading to available sources, concrete examples, counterexamples, domain knowledge, and the author's judgment. This does not require a new experiment each time. Separate what the material supports, what the analyst infers, and what is proposed as composition.

For structural candidates, show what they explain, which distinctions changed, what remains unexplained, and how the reading informs the current choice or composition. Make reasoned qualitative judgments; select a recommendation when adoption judgment is delegated.

Framework-derived placements are `framework_generated`. Preserve that origin even if independent target support is later obtained. Adoption, affinity grouping, and agreement across frameworks do not add independent support.

## Optional record

Prose suffices for small tasks. When the comparison rationale needs to remain traceable, record the following as useful, without treating them as a fixed schema or mandatory input form.

```text
Purpose and comparison units:
Current understanding and difficult distinctions:
Framework, lineage, distinctions or operations used:
Adaptation for this target and its judging subject:
Readings, reasons, and competing readings by target:
Relations preserved or changed across perspectives:
Target support, counterexamples, and undecided material:
Structural candidate in target language:
Recommended use and reasons:
Remaining questions and readings not adopted:
```

Foreground the relations discovered about the target and their use rather than introducing the framework. Label explanatory examples as examples, and keep them separate from observations or evidence of efficacy.
