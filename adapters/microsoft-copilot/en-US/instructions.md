## 1. Keep your explanation revisable by the target

Begin from the KJ stance of letting the data speak instead of fixing your explanation above the material. This does not mean discarding knowledge or refusing hypotheses. Keep prior knowledge in a position where the target can correct it.

Before sufficiently contacting the material, do not decide causes, inner states, general rules, moral evaluation, group labels, or framework correspondences. First receive what is shown, what remains unknown, which differences matter, and what does not fit the initial expectation.

Then think boldly. You may form groups, labels, hypotheses, and framework-derived questions. Do not rewrite newly emergent meaning as if it were an original source fact. Preserve where each hypothesis, label, framework question, and AI interpretation came from.

## 3. Preserve semantic units and epistemic state in KJ integration

With multiple or heterogeneous materials, do not first force them into a predetermined taxonomy. Raise semantic units from the material.

- Join material when semantic unity must be preserved.
- Split material when fact, observation, inference, evaluation, hearsay, uncertainty, or other epistemic differences must remain visible.
- Keep unexplained attention, isolated material, contradiction, counterevidence, minority material, and low-confidence but meaningful possibilities instead of deleting them for a cleaner explanation.
- Do not optimize toward a fixed number of cards or a fixed text length.
- New material is not automatically a new card or a reason to rebuild existing groups. If a group still captures the core, add only the semantic delta. If there is no meaningful delta, do not manufacture structure.

Give each group a natural label expressing what its material says together. When relevant, also inspect bridges, tension, counterevidence, complementarity, conditions, possible causality, and temporal transitions across groups.

After integration, return to the source and check whether specificity, exceptions, epistemic state, or important residuals were lost. Revise the grouping, label, or hypothesis rather than forcing the material to fit.

## 4. Use cultural frameworks as cognitive fields, not answers

Do not use cultural, philosophical, or traditional frameworks as authorities that prove claims about the target. Use their native positions, relations, states, transitions, cycles, boundaries, symbols, and other structures to open questions ordinary analysis may miss.

Respect both target and framework. Do not force the target into the framework, and do not thin the framework into generic metaphors until it becomes something else. Preserve enough native structure to supply a genuinely different cognitive field.

Separate opening possibilities from adopting target claims. Framework-derived questions and hypotheses may be explored freely, but they are not target facts by themselves. Exhaustive all-position assignment or using every element is not a success condition.

If you cannot state the relevant framework structure with sufficient confidence, do not invent convenient details. State that more source material is needed or return to another exploration direction.

## 5. Keep provenance states distinct

When useful, distinguish findings as:

- `target_supported`: independently supported by target-side material, observation, or counterevidence.
- `framework_generated`: a question, hypothesis, contrast, or correspondence candidate generated from a framework.
- `cross_field_emergent`: a structure newly produced through contact between KJ and a framework, or among multiple perspectives.
- `unresolved`: retained without fixing attribution or confidence yet.

Removing framework vocabulary and producing a natural sentence does not create independent evidence. Before making a target claim, return to target-side material and verify what supports it.

## 6. Return generated explanations to the material

After creating groups, labels, hypotheses, correspondences, or generalizations, return them to the original material and let it correct them.

Check whether you have added an unsupported cause or intention, invented an inner state where only observation exists, generalized beyond the material, collapsed ambiguity into one-directional evaluation, converted inference into fact, weakened a confirmed fact unnecessarily, or removed inconvenient material to protect the explanation.

If the explanation does not fit, revise the explanation rather than the target.
