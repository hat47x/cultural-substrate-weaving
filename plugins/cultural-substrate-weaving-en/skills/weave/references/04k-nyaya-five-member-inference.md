# Nyāya five-member inference — thesis, reason, corroboration, application, conclusion

## Structural core adopted by CSW

CSW uses the five-member presentation of argument / inference for others associated with early Nyāya as a cognitive resource.

The working sequence is:

1. **pratijñā** — the thesis to be established;
2. **hetu** — the offered reason;
3. **udāharaṇa / dṛṣṭānta** — a corroborating or recognized example that exposes the proposed connection;
4. **upanaya** — application of that connection to the present target;
5. **nigamana** — the conclusion restated after application.

The familiar smoke/fire example presents a claim about fire on a hill, smoke as the reason, a recognized smoke–fire case, application to the hill, and the conclusion.

CSW borrows the operation of **making the inferential bridge inspectable**. It does not treat filling five slots as success.

## Strong cognitive operations

- thesis/reason split;
- corroborating-example probe;
- rule-to-case application check;
- inferential-bridge exposure;
- positive/negative comparison when useful;
- pseudo-reason audit;
- debate-facing restatement.

### Operation IDs used by the selection workspace

The research inventory groups the operations above under these stable IDs:

- `argument-unfolding`: unfold the five members and restate them in a traceable order;
- `reason-rule-separation`: separate the observed reason from the general relation used to reach the conclusion;
- `example-counterexample-probe`: use corroborating and dissimilar cases to inspect the relation's scope;
- `rule-application-audit`: inspect whether the recognized relation applies to the present target;
- `inference-gap-detection`: preserve missing bridges, extra assumptions, and pseudo-reasons as residuals.

## Questions returned to the target

- What exactly is the thesis?
- Which target-side observation is functioning as the reason?
- What recognized case is supposed to establish the relevant connection?
- Does the example show the needed relation, or only a superficial resemblance?
- What extra assumption enters when the relation is applied to this target?
- What counterexample or dissimilar case narrows the reason's scope?
- After Nyāya vocabulary is removed, can a reader still trace thesis → reason → example → application → conclusion?

## Distinction from other adopted frameworks

- Aristotle's four causes pluralizes kinds of explanation; Nyāya inspects the inferential bridge from a reason to a claim.
- Catuṣkoṭi opens a proposition beyond a binary frame; Nyāya asks which resulting claim is actually supported and how.
- Jain sevenfold predication conditions assertions by standpoint; Nyāya externalizes the warrant/application sequence.

## Preserve the framework's specificity

- Do not identify the five members with an “Indian version” of Aristotelian syllogism.
- Do not collapse Nyāya, Buddhist logic, Vaiśeṣika, Caraka debate forms, and later Navya-Nyāya into one invariant system.
- Completing all five members does not establish validity or truth.
- A corroborating example is not automatically independent target evidence.
- Do not use the stock smoke/fire case as a license for free analogy across unrelated domains.
- Do not erase the historical epistemic/debate setting by translating anumāna or pramāṇa into a generic proof pipeline.

## Current scope

This dossier centers the early Nyāya five-member inferential presentation.

Later theories of inference, detailed accounts of vyāpti and hetvābhāsa, Navya-Nyāya terminology, and other Indian logical traditions require separate lineage-specific work.

## Exploration and attribution

For exploration, the five-member structure can reveal a missing warrant, a merely illustrative example, or an application step that was hidden by fluent prose.

A clean five-member argument is not target-side evidence that a causal law, generalization, or conclusion is true. Reasons, examples, and applicability must still survive target-return checking.

## De-binding

Before returning the result to the target:

1. remove Sanskrit labels unless needed for provenance;
2. restate thesis, reason, recognized example, application, and conclusion in target vocabulary;
3. preserve which bridge was framework-generated;
4. return the reason and example to target-side evidence;
5. keep failed applications and counterexamples as residuals rather than repairing the argument automatically.
