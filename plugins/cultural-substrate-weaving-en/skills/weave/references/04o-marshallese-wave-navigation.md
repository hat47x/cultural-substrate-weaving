# Marshallese wave navigation and stick charts

## Structural core adopted by CSW

Public sources on Marshallese navigation describe relations among islands, swell interactions, route cues, instructional models, memory, and embodied sensing. Stick charts are described as teaching or rehearsal models rather than literal scale maps read during a voyage; situated navigation depends on renewed sensing of the sea.

CSW adopts the distinction among relative route structure, preparatory model, cue sequence, and situated re-observation. It does not attempt to reconstruct family- or school-held navigational expertise.

## Strong CSW operations

- relative-route-model: represent a target through relative route cues without assuming a global coordinate system.
- cue-sequence-wayfinding: inspect the sequence of cues expected before the next decision.
- model-environment-return: separate what a model can prepare from what must be re-sensed in the live situation.
- disturbance-as-signal: ask whether a perturbation carries state or location information rather than being only noise.
- embodied-abstract-complementarity: keep abstract representation and situated sensing as distinct but cooperating information sources.

## Target return

Ask whether local cues or absolute coordinates fit the target better, what cue should appear before the next decision, what must be checked in the live environment, whether anomalies contain information, and whether a local observation can update the route without rebuilding the whole map.

## Limits

Do not treat stick charts as literal modern maps or one invariant grammar shared by all navigators, families, periods, or schools. Do not infer restricted knowledge from public records or present CSW as reproducing navigator expertise. Do not use the framework for real marine navigation or safety decisions.
