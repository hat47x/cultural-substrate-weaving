# Receive the target

Identify what the caller wants to understand, compare, create, or choose. Read the purpose and existing delegation, then make the required judgment concrete.

Briefly retain the current question, material references, current understanding, and preservation requirements in target language. A pre-contact account makes later changes explainable; it is not an experimental control group.

For a problem set, align what counts as a problem and the actors, situations, periods, and scales compared. Identify any analyst-added units or causal assumptions.

When material organization is needed, delegate to an available affinity-diagramming realization and receive its result as target material. Unorganized material and not-yet-articulated concerns can be valid starting points.

Separate facts, firsthand statements, others' interpretations, conjectures, and proposed creations. Compositions and qualitative judgments need not await empirical proof of every item. When judging from existing sources, domain knowledge, or the caller's experience, show the basis and scope.

Receive domain quality criteria, values, and deliverable format from the caller's context. Identify missing material and proceed with what the available material supports.
