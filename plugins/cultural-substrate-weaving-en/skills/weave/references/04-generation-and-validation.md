# 04 — Generation and Validation

Read this file when generating candidates and validating generative excess, target-side necessity, value, and resource consumption.

## 5. Generate candidates and validate them on the target

Expand candidates across qualitatively different domains such as objects, places, institutions, bodies, actions, and time. Include relationships in which elements change one another's states. Look for integrated elements that realize multiple functions through one action or mechanism.

Give each element a role required inside the target in addition to its assigned use. Preserve room for unanticipated uses, meanings, and relationships. Higher generative excess, autonomy, and regenerative capacity broaden use situations and additional value.

For each candidate, examine whether it:

- has a reason to be necessary inside the target;
- produces understanding or judgment beyond the baseline;
- explains its difference from existing theory;
- generates a target-specific relationship beyond correspondence with a framework.

Present results as target-specific judgments, relationships, and procedures—not as correspondence tables with a framework.

Stance, framework, recipient position, reach, and format constrain one another. Choose the configuration that most broadly serves the target's purpose as a whole rather than maximizing each part independently.

When local polish conflicts with achievement of the target's overall purpose, record the side chosen and the side lost.

Evaluate high-load expression from the target as a whole.

- **Entry function:** Is it necessary to bring the recipient into the target?
- **Ability to disengage:** Is there a route for the recipient to take distance?
- **Destination:** Does the target as a whole show where it leads?
- **Precision:** Is the load limited to the level needed for the purpose?

Use checks as directional aids against oversight, not as scales for scoring the target's value. Fixed compliance scorecards tend to treat target-specific exceptions as defects.

After checking individual items, review the target as a whole. Reject candidates that leave unnatural uniformity, erase target-specific variation, or produce excessive coherence.

Record both generated value and consumed resources.

- **Generated value:** generative excess, autonomy, regenerative capacity, understanding, capability, reusable problem patterns.
- **Consumed resources:** participant and recipient attention, psychological capacity, work time, operational load, and follow-up cost.

Before adding content, separate two motivations.

- **Author-side need:** “what the author cannot resist saying.”
- **Target-side need:** “what the target must say.”

Then ask what the target's purpose would lose if the content were omitted. Recheck whether author-side need is being explained as target-side need. Retain candidates whose omission would produce a target-side loss.

Base judgments on facts and inferences you have examined. Validate trusted thinkers, existing theories, and organizational conventions against target-side material as well.

Before choosing among opposing views, identify the evaluation axes. Record each view's assumptions and scope separately. When simplifying or compressing information, state the purpose and the information lost. Mark provisional organization as provisional and attach conditions for reevaluation.
