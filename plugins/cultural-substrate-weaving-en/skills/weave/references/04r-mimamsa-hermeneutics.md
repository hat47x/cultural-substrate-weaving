# Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics for prescriptive text

## Structural core adopted by CSW

Mīmāṃsā developed detailed linguistic, hermeneutic, and deontic tools for interpreting prescriptive Vedic texts. CSW does not adopt Vedic authority or ritual obligation. It uses a bounded set of operations for determining prescriptive units, inspecting how words and clauses connect, separating explicit wording from contextual supplementation, and decomposing apparent conflicts among prescriptions.

## Strong CSW operations

- prescriptive-unit-detection: identify what belongs to one prescription and what should be separated.
- purpose-unity-probe: do not join adjacent clauses unless a shared action or purpose supports the unit.
- syntactic-expectancy-check: inspect which expressions require other expressions to complete the construction.
- semantic-fitness-check: reject combinations that are syntactically possible but semantically unworkable.
- explicit-vs-context-supplied-distinction: separate what is stated from what the reader supplies from context.
- norm-conflict-decomposition: ask whether two prescriptions concern the same agent, action, time, condition, and purpose before calling them contradictory.

## Bhāṭṭa / Prābhākara boundary

Mīmāṃsā does not contain one theory of sentence meaning.

Prābhākara accounts characteristically hold that words convey meanings already connected in context. Bhāṭṭa accounts characteristically hold that words first convey meanings and that those meanings are then connected into sentence meaning.

CSW does not decide this dispute. The runtime core uses only operations that can preserve the difference: syntactic expectancy, semantic fitness, cognitive proximity, explicit wording, and contextual supplementation.

## Target return

Ask where one prescription begins and ends, why adjacent clauses belong together, which terms syntactically require one another, whether the proposed connection is semantically workable, what is explicit versus supplied, and whether apparently conflicting rules actually govern the same agent, act, time, condition, and purpose.

## Near-neighbor boundary

Classical stasis theory locates the kind of issue in a dispute—fact, definition, evaluation, or procedure. Mīmāṃsā here inspects how a prescriptive text forms units and apparent obligations.

Nyāya five-member inference inspects the bridge from reason to conclusion. Mīmāṃsā here inspects how clauses, exceptions, contextual supplementation, and normative conditions connect.

## Limits

Do not transfer Vedic authority to unrelated documents or convert ritual prescriptions into modern legal, ethical, or organizational rules.

Do not collapse Bhāṭṭa and Prābhākara into one interpretation algorithm. Using these distinctions does not guarantee one uniquely correct reading. Where domain-specific law, contract, specification, or policy controls the interpretation, final judgment returns to that domain.
