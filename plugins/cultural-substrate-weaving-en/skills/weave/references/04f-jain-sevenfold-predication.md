# Jain anekāntavāda, syādvāda, and sevenfold predication

Jain anekāntavāda and syādvāda treat predication as conditioned by standpoint. Saptabhaṅgī organizes seven forms involving affirmation, negation, both, inexpressibility, and combinations. CSW uses it to expose omitted conditions, not as seven modern truth values.

Strong operations include perspective binding, qualified assertion, preserving inexpressible residuals, decomposing apparent contradiction by time/aspect/standpoint, and perspective rotation.

Do not reduce this to generic relativism or identify it with the Buddhist tetralemma. It does not assert seven ontological states in the target.
