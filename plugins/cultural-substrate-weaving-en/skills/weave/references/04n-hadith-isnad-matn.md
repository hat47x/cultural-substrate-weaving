# Hadith isnād / matn structure

## Structural core adopted by CSW

A hadith report is analytically divided into the substantive report, matn, and the attributed chain of transmission, isnād. CSW adopts the separation of content and transmission path, not a religious authenticity judgment.

When similar reports travel through multiple paths, chain divergence/convergence and textual variation can be compared separately. This prevents provenance prestige from silently becoming evidence for the truth of the transmitted proposition.

## Strong CSW operations

- transmission-content-separation: separate what is said from how it is said to have been transmitted.
- branch-and-convergence-comparison: compare multiple paths and where they diverge or share sources.
- attribution-path-audit: keep observed, asserted, missing, uncertain, and later-attributed links distinct.
- matn-variant-comparison: compare content variants independently of chain structure.
- provenance-content-non-equivalence: do not treat well-documented provenance as proof of the proposition.

## Target return

Ask where content claims and transmission claims diverge, where apparently independent paths share a source, which handoffs are directly documented, and whether wording variation and path variation correlate.

## Limits

Do not collapse classical hadith criticism and modern historical isnād-cum-matn analysis into one reliability algorithm. Do not reuse religious authenticity grades or narrator evaluation as general-purpose trust scores. The existence of an isnād does not itself establish historical truth.
