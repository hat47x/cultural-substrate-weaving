# Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics target-return example

Status: hypothetical / profile-readiness support

A deployment policy says: “For production changes, obtain approval. Emergency changes may proceed immediately when service restoration cannot wait. Record the reason afterward.”

A Mīmāṃsā-inspired pass does not import Vedic authority. It asks how the prescriptive text is segmented and connected.

- Is “record the reason afterward” part of the emergency exception or a general rule?
- Which words syntactically expect one another?
- Which reading is semantically workable?
- What condition makes the exception apply?
- Do approval and emergency action truly conflict under the same agent, time, condition, and purpose?
- Which part of the interpretation is explicit, and which part is supplied by context?

The useful result is a traceable distinction among wording, syntactic connection, semantic fit, contextual supplementation, and apparent norm conflict. The religious source of the interpretive operations is retained as provenance but does not become authority over the deployment policy.
