# Confucian role / li target-return worked example

Status: hypothetical / research support for runtime adoption

A project incident is described as “the owner failed to decide.”

The role name alone does not establish what happened. A role/li pass separates the formal title, the relation to other participants, the interaction pattern in which decisions are normally made, and the observed behavior.

The pass may reveal that the nominal owner lacked decision authority while another role routinely made the relevant decisions. The useful target question is then whether the conflict comes from a mismatch between title, enacted function, and expected relationship.

The historical Confucian norm is removed before returning to the target. No conclusion is drawn that one role ought to dominate another. What survives is a role–relation–behavior distinction that can be checked against the project's actual records.


## Second target: patterned release-meeting interaction

A hypothetical release process has an explicit accountable owner and correct formal consultations. In every weekly review, the lead engineer recommends a choice before the facilitator asks, "Any objections?" The team later reports some concerns privately rather than at the public closing question.

This alone does **not** prove that the speaking sequence caused suppression.

A bounded role/li pass asks whether the repeated public sequence has become an enacted signal that the decision is closed and whether the same words would invite a different response before the lead recommendation.

### Return to target evidence

- Preserve the formal authority and consultation records separately from how the invitation was performed.
- Inspect meeting order, words, pauses, who spoke, and when concerns appeared elsewhere.
- Ask participants privately how they understood the invitation; do not infer mental states from silence.
- Compare meetings with early independent objections against the current sequence where feasible.
- Consider alternative causes: issue criticality, deadline pressure, retaliation fear, information delay, or simply no concerns.

The framework-generated result is a **testable question about interaction context**. It is not proof that ritual caused silence or a norm that members must defer to leaders. If routine-analysis or team practice review already opens this question, non-activation is valid.

This second fixture separates discovery about enacted interaction from the first fixture's routine RACI/authority mismatch, which ordinary project review can reveal before framework contact.
