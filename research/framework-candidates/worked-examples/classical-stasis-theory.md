# Classical stasis theory worked examples

Status: hypothetical / research support for runtime adoption

## Positive example

After a release incident, one participant argues that rollback never happened. Another says that what happened should not count as a rollback. A third accepts the event but argues it was justified. A fourth argues that only the incident commander was authorized to decide.

A stasis pass separates four live questions: fact, definition, evaluation, and competence/procedure. Different evidence is needed for each. Timeline logs cannot by themselves settle whether the action was justified, while a policy document does not establish what actually occurred.

## Negative example

Two designers disagree over which visual treatment “feels calmer,” but neither side is making a stable factual, definitional, evaluative-with-shared-criteria, or procedural claim. Forcing the discussion into the four-way minimum adds labels without clarifying the target.

The correct CSW result is to reject the stasis pass for that case and preserve the unresolved experiential disagreement.
