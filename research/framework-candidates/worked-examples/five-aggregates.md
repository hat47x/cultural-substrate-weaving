# Five aggregates target-return example

Status: hypothetical / profile-readiness support

An interview participant says:

> “The system rejected me, I panicked, and I knew I would fail.”

A five-aggregates-inspired pass does not diagnose the participant and does not decide that each clause corresponds to a Buddhist category.

It opens separate target questions:

- What material or bodily event actually occurred?
- What unpleasant feeling was reported before interpretation?
- What recognition or interpretation produced “the system rejected me”?
- What action tendency or learned disposition appeared with the panic?
- What was directly noticed or attended to at the time?
- Which of these distinctions are supported by the interview, logs, or observation, and which are only framework-generated?

The useful output is not a five-box classification. It is a less reified account in which feeling, interpretation, action tendency, and awareness are no longer automatically treated as one stable property of the person.
