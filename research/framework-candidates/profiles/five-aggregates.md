# Five aggregates candidate profile

Status: sourced-candidate / research-only

## Source basis

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Mind in Indian Buddhist Philosophy

https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Abhidharma

https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/abhidharma/

The sources present five aggregates as one Buddhist analysis of sentient experience and note that Buddhist analysis also uses other schemes such as sense fields and elements.

## Structural value for CSW

The five aggregates can force a coarse “person/experience” statement into different process categories: form, sensation, perception/apperception, formations/volitions, and consciousness.

## Candidate operations

experience-decomposition, anti-agent-reification, process-view, component-interaction, residual-after-decomposition.

## Boundaries

Do not call this a modern clinical psychology taxonomy. Do not use Buddhist claims about self/personhood as facts about a target. Preserve differences among Buddhist analytical traditions.

## Target-return questions

- Which part of this account is sensation versus interpretation?
- Is a disposition being mistaken for immediate perception?
- What remains after the unitary “self” label is removed from the description?

## Adoption gap

Needs a sharper non-overlap test against dependent origination and Taiheki before runtime adoption.
