# Five aggregates candidate profile

Status: profile-ready / research-only / Buddhist-tradition-sensitive

## Identity

- names: 五蘊, five aggregates, pañcaskandha / pañcakkhandha
- current scope: a bounded early-Buddhist / Abhidharma-informed decomposition of sentient experience into form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness
- intended CSW use: experience decomposition, anti-reification, process view, and residual inspection
- not intended use: diagnosis, personality typing, modern clinical psychology, or importing Buddhist claims about selfhood into a target

## Structural core

The useful CSW operation is not “everything can be sorted into five boxes.”

It is to interrupt a unitary account of a person or experience and ask whether materially different processes are being collapsed:

- bodily/material form;
- affective feeling or valence;
- perception / recognition / interpretation;
- formations, dispositions, intentions, or action tendencies;
- consciousness or awareness.

The categories are used as prompts for separation, not as target-side facts.

## Native operation candidates

### experience-decomposition

Split one coarse account of experience into materially different process questions.

### anti-agent-reification

Ask whether a statement attributes one stable agent-property to what may instead be a changing collection of processes.

### process-view

Reframe a static “person is X” statement into events or processes that arise, change, and cease.

### component-interaction

Ask which distinctions interact without assuming one component alone explains the whole experience.

### residual-after-decomposition

Preserve what does not map cleanly instead of forcing every target observation into one of the five labels.

## Target-return questions

- Which part of the account is bodily/material condition, feeling, recognition/interpretation, disposition/action tendency, or awareness?
- Is a feeling being reported as if it were already an interpretation?
- Is an interpretation being mistaken for a stable trait of the person?
- Which part changes over time, and which part is only being narrated as stable?
- What target-side evidence supports each proposed distinction?
- What remains after the unitary person-label is removed?
- If Buddhist terminology is removed, do the process distinctions still help the target?

## Near-neighbor differentiation

### Five aggregates vs dependent origination

Dependent origination asks which conditions make phenomena arise or cease.

Five-aggregate decomposition asks what kinds of process are being collapsed inside an account of experience.

A feeling can be distinguished from a perception without yet asserting a causal chain between them.

### Five aggregates vs Taiheki

Taiheki contributes embodied observational axes and patterned bodily response.

Five aggregates are not a body-type or personality system. Their useful CSW role is to separate dimensions inside a moment or stream of experience, not to classify a person.

### Five aggregates vs modern psychology

Do not rename the five aggregates as modern psychological faculties and then claim scientific equivalence. Any target-side psychological interpretation must come from target-appropriate evidence.

## De-binding route

1. remove Buddhist claims about no-self, liberation, rebirth, and soteriology from the target-facing result;
2. translate the five aggregates only into provisional process questions;
3. mark each proposed separation as `framework_generated` until the target supports it;
4. do not require every observation to fit one of five categories;
5. preserve overlap or residual material;
6. reject the framework when the target is not an account of sentient experience or self-description.
