## Structural core retained by this profile

### Whole and part are co-defining

Fazang's rafter/building analysis treats the identity of a part as dependent on the whole in which it functions, while the whole also depends on its parts.

Cognitive contribution:

- do not assume that decomposing a whole reveals more fundamental independent units;
- ask how a component's role changes when the containing whole changes;
- ask which supposedly local property exists only because of system-level participation.

### Mutual inclusion without erasing difference

Huayan arguments preserve both differentiation and mutual dependence.

For CSW, the useful move is not to say that all things are simply identical. It is to hold together:

- part and whole;
- identity and difference;
- integration and separateness;
- influence and vulnerability.

This can expose explanations that become reductive when they choose only one side.

### Network perspective

Indra's Net is one metaphor for mutual dependence and reflection across a whole field.

For CSW, treat it as one illustrative device rather than as the entire Huayan system.

Cognitive contribution:

- pick one node and ask what relations become visible from that position;
- ask how changing the role of one node changes the apparent identity of others;
- compare local and whole-field descriptions without assuming either is independently complete.

### Role-defined identity

A rafter outside a building may no longer function or be identified as a rafter.

Cognitive contribution:

- distinguish intrinsic-looking labels from context-dependent roles;
- ask what remains invariant when an element moves to another whole;
- identify classifications that are secretly relational.

## Native operation candidates

### whole-part-reciprocity

Inspect a target element and the larger structure that gives it a role.

Questions:

- What does the whole require from this part?
- What makes this part the kind of part we say it is?
- Would its identity change outside this whole?

Outputs:

- whole-part distinction
- relation candidate
- context-dependence question

### mutual-inclusion-view

Hold two or more elements as distinct while asking how each is partly defined through the others.

Questions:

- Which relation is necessary for the current identities to make sense?
- What appears independent only because the surrounding network has been hidden?
- Which difference must remain visible even while integration is acknowledged?

Outputs:

- network relation candidate
- preserved difference
- residual

### perspective-through-node

Select one node and reread the larger field from that position.

Questions:

- Which parts of the whole become salient from here?
- Which relations disappear from another node's viewpoint?
- Does the apparent centrality belong to the target or to the chosen perspective?

Outputs:

- counter-view
- perspective distinction
- question

### context-role-reidentification

Move a component hypothetically into a different containing whole or remove its current role.

Questions:

- What remains the same?
- What label ceases to fit?
- Which target claim depended on the original context?

Outputs:

- falsifier
- transition candidate
- reframing question

### integration-with-difference

When a synthesis says that several parts form one whole, explicitly ask what must not be collapsed.

Outputs:

- preserved difference
- residual
- revised synthesis candidate

## What not to import by default

- Huayan metaphysics as a target-domain fact;
- the claim that every target element is empirically connected to every other element;
- Indra's Net as a complete summary of Huayan;
- a generic network-science model presented as if it were Huayan doctrine;
- the idea that mutual dependence means equal causal strength;
- Korean Hwaeom, Japanese Kegon, and Chinese Huayan as one invariant lineage;
- religious or soteriological conclusions into unrelated domains.

## Historical/lineage caution

This profile is centered on Chinese Huayan and especially Fazang because that is where the present source basis is strongest.

Before profile-ready status, distinguish at least:

- Avatamsaka Sutra background;
- Fazang's specific whole-part and mutual-inclusion arguments;
- later Chinese Huayan developments;
- Korean Hwaeom and Japanese Kegon receptions where used.

Do not back-project a later diagram, metaphor, or scholastic formulation into a single timeless system.

## Exploratory prompts produced by this profile

- Is this component's identity actually independent of the whole around it?
- If this part were moved into another context, which properties would disappear?
- What does the current whole look like from this one node?
- Which relation is being hidden by treating components as self-contained?
- Can integration be described without erasing the differences that still matter?
- Does the current analysis privilege the whole so much that local resistance disappears?
- Does it privilege isolated parts so much that role-dependent identity disappears?

## De-binding

Before returning a candidate to the target:

1. remove Huayan, Indra's Net, rafter, and Buddhist metaphysical vocabulary unless needed for provenance;
2. restate the candidate as a target-side whole-part, relation, perspective, or context-dependence question;
3. keep framework_generated origin;
4. identify what target-side source/card would support or push back on the relation;
5. retain differences that resist whole-field integration;
6. use target-return audit if the candidate survives only after weakening or reframing.
