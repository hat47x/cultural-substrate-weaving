Status: sourced-candidate / lineage-sensitive / ritual-sensitive

https://dev.rubinmuseum.org/images/content/Rubin_Digital_Resource_Creative_Constructions.pdf

These sources show recurring Tibetan Buddhist mandala features such as a center, directional differentiation, palace/gateway structure, concentric or enclosing boundaries, ritual/visualization use, and in some practices movement conceptually from outside toward the center. Particular deity mandalas, schools, initiation rules, colors, directions, and ritual sequences vary and must not be collapsed into one timeless diagram.

## Structural value for CSW

The distinctive contribution is **spatial organization with thresholds** rather than a generic hierarchy.

Potentially useful operations include:

- center / periphery distinction;
- four-direction differentiation;
- gated-threshold inspection;
- nested-enclosure reading;
- outside-to-center traversal;
- protected/intermediate zones;
- construction-order awareness;
- creation / dissolution contrast where a documented sand-mandala practice supports it.

This can fill the current spatial center-periphery gap while also supplying a ritualized route through bounded space.

## Target-return questions

- What is currently being treated as the center, and what evidence makes it central?
- What changes when the same field is read from a direction or gateway rather than from the center?
- Which boundary separates “outside” from “inside,” and what must happen to cross it?
- Are there intermediate enclosures that are being skipped by a flat diagram?
- Is an apparently peripheral element actually guarding or mediating entry?
- Would an outside-to-center traversal expose a sequence that a static hierarchy misses?
- If mandala vocabulary is removed, do center, threshold, direction, enclosure, and traversal questions survive in target language?

## Living-tradition and lineage boundary

- Do not treat “the Tibetan mandala” as one invariant geometry.
- Do not universalize one deity mandala's colors, deities, directions, gates, or correspondences.
- Initiation, visualization, consecration, and deity practice can require lineage-specific authority; CSW must not simulate that authority.
- A museum diagram is not a substitute for ritual transmission.
- Do not convert sacred spatial correspondences into facts about an unrelated target.
- Do not imply that moving toward a diagrammatic center is inherently spiritually or psychologically superior in the target.

## De-binding route

1. keep only abstract spatial operations that survive without deity/color/cosmological correspondence;
2. restate center, direction, threshold, enclosure, traversal, and sequence in target vocabulary;
3. record that the spatial candidate came from a mandala framework;
4. return every target-side center/boundary claim to independent evidence;
5. preserve alternate centers, contested boundaries, and non-traversable gaps as residuals.

## Adoption gap

The structural value is high, but runtime adoption should first define a deliberately narrow lineage/usage scope. At minimum, a dossier must separate general mandala geometry from deity-specific iconography, initiation practice, school-specific correspondences, and modern museum pedagogy.
