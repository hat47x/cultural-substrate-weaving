# Rasa framework profile candidate

Status: sourced-candidate / research-only

## Identity

- names: Rasa / ラサ / Indian aesthetic rasa theory
- family: South Asian aesthetic theory
- intended CSW use: composition-to-reception transformation
- current profile scope: aesthetic experience and reception structure; not personality classification

## Source basis

Oxford Bibliographies, Rasa in Indian Aesthetics

https://academic.oup.com/reference/62382/reference-article-abstract/566568637

The reference describes rasa as a cornerstone of Indian aesthetics, rooted in the Nāṭyaśāstra and developed extensively by later thinkers including Abhinavagupta. It emphasizes aesthetic savor/experience rather than a simple list of emotions.

Oxford Bibliographies, Aesthetics

https://academic.oup.com/reference/62357/reference-article-abstract/554506345

This source situates the Nāṭyaśāstra as a major early source for rasa theory and emphasizes the long history of interpretation rather than one closed modern taxonomy.

## Structural core retained by this profile

### Artifact is not experience

The emotional or expressive material represented by an artwork is not identical to the aesthetic experience that arises for a receiver.

This distinction is the core cognitive contribution to CSW.

### Composition

Aesthetic effect emerges through a composition of represented situations, affective materials, performance/artifact features, and reception.

The minimal profile therefore treats rasa as a transformation question:

source material / expression
→ composition
→ receiver encounter
→ aesthetic experience

It does not reduce the process to one emotion label.

### Spectator position

The receiver is structurally important.

This opens a perspective unavailable to analyses that examine only author intent or artifact content.

## Native operation candidates

### reception-view

Re-describe the target from the position of a receiver rather than producer.

Questions:

- What is present in the artifact?
- What experience is made possible but not literally stated?
- Which effect depends on the receiver's position?

Outputs:

- audience question
- experiential distinction
- residual

### composition-to-experience

Trace how multiple elements combine into an emergent experience.

Outputs:

- transformation candidate
- relation candidate
- composition question

Boundary:

Do not claim a specific rasa category unless the historical/category layer has actually been sourced and applied.

### split-representation-from-effect

Separate:

- represented emotion;
- formal device;
- contextual cue;
- receiver experience.

This operation is useful for fiction, interface design, communication, ritual, performance, and other artifacts where effect cannot be read directly from surface content.

### counter-receiver

Ask how the same artifact changes under a different receiver position or competence.

Outputs:

- counter-view
- boundary question
- accessibility residual

## What not to import by default

- a modern nine-emotion checklist as the entire tradition;
- a universal theory of human emotion;
- psychological diagnosis;
- claims that every receiver experiences the same rasa;
- later theological or metaphysical interpretations without lineage labels.

## Historical/lineage caution

Rasa theory developed over centuries.

Before skill-candidate status:

- distinguish the Nāṭyaśāstra formulation from later elaborations;
- document the relation between rasa and bhāva terminology;
- treat the later prominence of śānta and navarasa formulations historically rather than as an invariant starting point;
- add at least one source that supports a concrete operation beyond general overview.

## Exploratory prompts produced by this profile

- What is represented, and what is actually experienced?
- Which effect only emerges from the combination of several elements?
- Does the artifact say one thing while making another feeling available?
- Which receiver assumptions are required for the effect?
- What changes for a receiver with different context or competence?
- Is an emotion label hiding a transformation process?

## De-binding

1. remove rasa category names unless they are needed and sourced;
2. express the result in terms of artifact features and receiver experience;
3. preserve framework_generated origin;
4. ask for target-side evidence from actual reception when available;
5. retain mismatch between intended and observed effect as a residual rather than forcing the theory to fit.

## Candidate adoption note

Current status remains sourced-candidate.

The framework adds a cognitive function currently weak in CSW: systematic separation of expression from reception. More primary/theoretical detail is needed before independent Skill adoption.
