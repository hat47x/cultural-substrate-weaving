# Buddhist mandala spatial-organization candidate profile

Status: sourced-candidate / research-only / lineage-sensitive

## Source basis

Museum and scholarly materials show that Buddhist mandalas can organize a ritual/visualized space around a center, directional quadrants, gateways, enclosing rings or walls, and graded movement from outside toward a central palace or deity. The exact iconography, deity set, ritual use, and spatial details vary by text, lineage, region, and historical period.

## Structural value for CSW

The distinctive contribution is **spatial organization with explicit center, periphery, directions, gates, nested boundaries, and approach paths**. It can expose structure that ordinary hierarchy or graph language misses.

Potential operations:

- center-periphery pass;
- directional-quadrant pass;
- gate / threshold probe;
- concentric-boundary pass;
- protected-center / mediated-access probe;
- outer-to-inner path;
- spatial-role reassignment;
- center-shift counter-view.

The cognitive value lies in spatial relations and access structure, not in copying iconography.

## Lineage / variant boundary

"Mandala" is not one invariant diagram.

- Tibetan Vajrayāna palace mandalas, Japanese Esoteric Buddhist mandalas, Pure Land visual schemes, and other Buddhist forms differ.
- Hindu yantra and mandala traditions must not be silently merged into Buddhist mandala structure.
- Deity correspondences, colors, directions, gates, rings, initiation procedures, and contemplative sequences are text- and lineage-sensitive.
- A museum object demonstrates a historically situated configuration, not a universal canonical template.

Runtime adoption should therefore either choose a bounded lineage/use-case or preserve only the cross-source spatial minimum while marking all richer correspondences lineage-specific.

## What not to import

- Do not reduce mandala to generic symmetry, "sacred geometry," or a decorative radial chart.
- Do not assign target elements to deities, directions, colors, elements, or enlightened qualities without an independent assignment procedure.
- Do not infer that the target has one privileged metaphysical center.
- Do not treat outer layers as inferior merely because some ritual paths move inward.
- Do not translate protected gates or enclosing rings directly into security, organizational, or psychological facts.
- Do not erase ritual, contemplative, and religious functions when citing the historical source.

## De-binding route

Before returning a generated candidate to the target:

1. remove deity/color/cosmology vocabulary unless historically relevant to the target;
2. retain only center / periphery / direction / boundary / gate / layer / path distinctions that survive;
3. state whether a "center" is geometric, functional, attentional, institutional, or merely framework-generated;
4. ask what target-side relation makes a boundary or gate real;
5. allow multiple centers or no meaningful center if the target resists the model.

## Target-return questions

- What functions as the center, and is that centrality spatial, causal, attentional, or institutional?
- Which elements are peripheral because of distance, access, protection, or role?
- What must be crossed before an actor/item can enter another region?
- Are there nested boundaries rather than one inside/outside distinction?
- Which directions or sectors actually differ on the target side?
- Does approaching the center change what can be seen or done?
- If the presumed center is shifted to another node, which relations remain?
- After removing mandala vocabulary, which spatial distinctions still survive?

## Adoption gap

The candidate fills the spatial center-periphery gap at research level. Runtime adoption should wait for:

- a bounded lineage decision or explicit minimal-cross-lineage structural contract;
- one worked target-return example demonstrating that "center" is not automatically privileged or true;
- a clear distinction from Huayan's node/whole-part perspective and from future route/network frameworks such as Jingluo.
