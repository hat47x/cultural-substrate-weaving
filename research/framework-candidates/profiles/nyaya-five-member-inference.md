# Nyāya five-member inference candidate profile

Status: adopted / runtime-corpus

## Source basis

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Logic in Classical Indian Philosophy

https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-india/

Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Nyaya

https://iep.utm.edu/nyaya/

Cambridge University Press, Roy W. Perrett, *An Introduction to Indian Philosophy*, chapter “Reasoning”

https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/an-introduction-to-indian-philosophy/reasoning/43A57DB83FF080BE57958897B4A7F6A2

Early Nyāya presents an inference for others / debate in five members: pratijñā (thesis), hetu (reason), dṛṣṭānta or udāharaṇa (corroborating example), upanaya (application), and nigamana (conclusion). Modern summaries often render the stock smoke/fire argument in that order. The sources also stress that Indian inference is tied to an epistemic process and debate practice, not merely to a Western syllogistic template.

## Structural value for CSW

The distinctive operation is **not** simply “make an argument with five bullets.” The useful structure is that a claim must expose:

1. what is being asserted;
2. what reason is offered;
3. what example or recognized case supports the claimed connection;
4. how that connection actually applies to the target case;
5. what conclusion follows after the application.

This is useful when a fluent explanation hides a missing bridge between a reason and the concrete target.

## Candidate operations

- thesis-reason separation;
- example/corroboration probe;
- rule-to-case application check;
- inferential-bridge exposure;
- positive/negative comparison prompt;
- reason-quality / pseudo-reason audit;
- debate-facing restatement.

## Distinction from adopted frameworks

- Aristotle's four causes pluralizes kinds of “why”; Nyāya inspects the inferential bridge from reason to claim.
- Catuṣkoṭi disrupts a binary proposition-space; Nyāya asks whether a proposed inference is actually supported.
- Jain sevenfold predication conditions assertions by standpoint; Nyāya makes the warrant/application structure explicit.

## Target-return questions

- What exactly is the thesis, stripped of explanation?
- What target-side observation is doing the work as the reason?
- Which familiar or independently accepted case demonstrates the claimed connection?
- Has the example only illustrated a resemblance, or does the relevant relation actually apply here?
- What changes if a counterexample or dissimilar case is introduced?
- Does the conclusion merely repeat the thesis, or has the intervening warrant survived target-side checking?
- If Nyāya vocabulary is removed, is there still an inspectable claim–reason–example–application chain?

## Boundaries

- Do not equate the five-member presentation with one timeless form of all Indian logic.
- Do not flatten Nyāya, Buddhist logic, Vaiśeṣika, Caraka debate traditions, and later Navya-Nyāya into one invariant system.
- Do not assume the five-member form by itself makes an argument valid.
- Do not treat a vivid example as independent evidence when it merely illustrates the proposed relation.
- Do not translate anumāna/pramāṇa into a generic modern “proof pipeline” without preserving the epistemic/debate context.
- Do not use the stock smoke/fire example as a license to analogize unrelated domains.

## De-binding route

Before returning an exploratory Nyāya pass to the target:

1. remove Sanskrit labels unless needed for provenance;
2. restate thesis, reason, example, application, and conclusion in target vocabulary;
3. mark which link was framework-generated;
4. ask whether the reason and example are independently supported by target-side material;
5. preserve failed applications or counterexamples as residuals rather than repairing the argument automatically.

### Capability-overlap comparison

See:

- `research/framework-candidates/comparisons/nyaya-five-member-inference-vs-toulmin-argument-review.md`

Toulmin-style argument review can reproduce much of the de-bound capability by separating claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, and rebuttal. Nyāya therefore does not need runtime retention on the claim that no other method can represent the inferential bridge.

### Discovery-value comparison

See:

- `research/framework-candidates/comparisons/nyaya-five-member-inference-discovery-value.md`

The ex-ante comparison uses ordinary incident/RCA review without naming Nyāya or argument mapping.

That baseline naturally asks for root causes, evidence, contributing factors, reproduction, elimination, and corrective action. It does not, however, reliably force a three-way separation between:

- the observation functioning as a reason;
- the generalized relation that licenses the inference;
- application of that relation to the current target.

Nyāya contact makes this bridge explicit and turns analogy or warrant overreach into an inspectable target-side question.

### Near-neighbor comparison

See:

- `research/framework-candidates/comparisons/mimamsa-vs-stasis-vs-nyaya.md`

Stasis locates the kind of issue under dispute; Mīmāṃsā scopes prescriptive text; Nyāya inspects the reason-to-conclusion bridge. Near-neighbor distinctness is preserved without using it as the sole retention argument.

### Requalification result

Runtime retention is supported under the discovery-aware standard.

The reason is **discovery contribution**, not unique formal capability.

Retain Nyāya when the missing cognitive job is an unstated warrant, an analogy leap, or an unchecked application of a general relation to the present target.

Do not activate it when a direct target-side intervention already settles the claim or when the target already makes claim, warrant, application conditions, and counterexamples explicit.

The minimal runtime dossier remains bounded to argument unfolding, reason/rule separation, example/counterexample probing, application audit, and inference-gap detection. It does not treat the five-member form as a universal truth validator or as the whole of Indian logic.
