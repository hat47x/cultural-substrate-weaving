# Jo-Ha-Kyū candidate profile

Status: sourced-candidate / research-only

## Source basis

J-STAGE, Characteristics of actions for sliding walk technique in Japanese traditional performing art

https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/hcs/2014/24/2014_204/_article/-char/en

The study analyzes Jo-Ha-Kyū in Kyogen walking and reports non-monotonic tempo shaped by acceleration and deceleration rather than three equal blocks.

J-STAGE / Journal of Architecture and Planning, RELATION BETWEEN TRANSITIONS IN THE NOH STAGE STYLE AFTER ZEAMI AND “JOHAKYU FIVE PARTS”

https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aija/81/728/81_2317/_article

This study describes Zeami's jo-ha-kyū five-part formulation as a grammar of Noh and reports correlations among Noh-stage areas, dancing, musical accompaniment, and jo-ha-kyū structure.

the-Noh.com, Johakyū — Noh Terminology

https://db2.the-noh.com/edic/2009/06/johakyu_1.html

The terminology entry describes jo as the introductory portion, ha as the unfolding middle, and kyū as the more energetic denouement, while noting that the principle also structures chant order, bridgeway speed, and even program order.

## Structural value for CSW

Jo-Ha-Kyū can supply temporal articulation: an opening condition, a breaking/developing acceleration, and a rapid or resolving phase. Its value is pacing and transition shape, not a three-item taxonomy.

## Candidate operations

pacing-segmentation, tempo-transition, acceleration-profile, closure-shape, nested-temporal-structure.

## Boundaries

Do not infer one universal numerical timing law from one performance study.

Keep at least these distinctions visible:

- Zeami/Noh-specific formulation vs later use of jo-ha-kyū in other arts;
- three named macro-phases vs Zeami's more detailed five-part articulation;
- pacing / temporal function vs a generic three-category taxonomy;
- performance description vs universal prescription for unrelated processes.

Broader use across music, tea, architecture, organization, or other domains needs source-specific work.

## Target-return questions

- Where does the process actually accelerate?
- Is the closing phase merely shorter, or structurally different?
- Does equal partitioning erase the temporal function of the phases?
