# Jo-Ha-Kyū candidate profile

Status: profile-ready / research-only / Zeami-Noh-bounded

## Identity

- names: 序破急, Jo-Ha-Kyū, Jyo-Ha-Kyu
- current scope: Zeami/Noh-centered temporal articulation, including the later documented jo-ha-kyū five-part formulation
- intended CSW use: pacing segmentation, tempo transition, acceleration profile, closure shape, and nested temporal structure
- not intended use: a universal Japanese timing law, three equal phases, or automatic prescription for unrelated processes

## Source basis

### Historical-text edition / commentary witness

National Diet Library, *Zeami Jūrokubushū Hyōshaku* (世阿弥十六部集評釈), upper volume

https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/book/1125601?page=1

The digitized edition/commentary preserves discussion of Noh's jo-ha-kyū and the five-part formulation: one jo, three ha, and one kyū. CSW treats this as a historical textual witness and commentary layer, not as an autograph manuscript.

### Scholarly Noh-stage study

J-STAGE / Journal of Architecture and Planning, “RELATION BETWEEN TRANSITIONS IN THE NOH STAGE STYLE AFTER ZEAMI AND ‘JOHAKYU FIVE PARTS’”

https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aija/81/728/81_2317/_article

This study describes Zeami's jo-ha-kyū five-part formulation as a grammar of Noh and examines relations among stage areas, dancing, and musical accompaniment.

### Empirical movement study

J-STAGE, “Characteristics of actions for sliding walk technique in Japanese traditional performing art”

https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/hcs/2014/24/2014_204/_article/-char/en

The study is useful because measured movement does not reduce jo-ha-kyū to three equal-duration blocks; acceleration and deceleration shape the temporal articulation.

### Tradition educational reference

the-Noh.com, Jo-Ha-Kyū / Noh terminology and performance explanations

https://www.the-noh.com/en/trivia/079.html

Useful for present-day Noh terminology and the relation between irregular timing and jo-ha-kyū. It is not used as a substitute for Zeami scholarship.

## Structural core

The useful CSW structure is **temporal function and tempo shape**, not a three-item taxonomy.

At a bounded minimum:

- jo establishes or prepares;
- ha unfolds, breaks, varies, or develops the established condition;
- kyū brings a more rapid or concentrated closing movement.

Zeami's Noh formulation can also articulate the overall structure as five parts: one jo, three ha, one kyū.

CSW does not convert these labels into fixed durations.

## Native operation candidates

### pacing-segmentation

Separate phases by temporal function instead of equal clock duration.

### tempo-transition

Ask where the rate or intensity of change shifts.

### acceleration-profile

Inspect acceleration and deceleration rather than only start/end timestamps.

### closure-shape

Ask whether closing is merely “the last third” or a structurally different concentration/rapid phase.

### nested-temporal-structure

Ask whether a larger jo-ha-kyū contains smaller pacing structures without assuming every target recursively follows the pattern.

## Target-return questions

- Where does the process actually change pace?
- Which phase establishes conditions, which develops or disrupts them, and which concentrates closure?
- Is the middle one uniform span, or does it contain materially different developments?
- Does equal partitioning hide the target's actual tempo?
- Is the final phase faster, denser, more decisive, or not structurally different at all?
- What target-side timing, event, or interaction data supports the proposed phase boundary?
- If Noh terminology is removed, does a distinctive temporal-shape question remain?

## Near-neighbor differentiation

### Jo-Ha-Kyū vs Maya calendars / periodic systems

Calendar frameworks expose recurrence, phase relation, and long time coordinates.

Jo-ha-kyū exposes **within-process pacing and tempo change**, not recurrence.

### Jo-Ha-Kyū vs rites of passage

Rites of passage asks whether separation, intermediate status, and incorporation are structurally distinct.

Jo-ha-kyū asks how pace and developmental function change across a process. A liminal state need not accelerate, and a kyū phase need not be an incorporation ritual.

### Jo-Ha-Kyū vs generic three-stage model

The framework is not useful if it merely renames beginning/middle/end. It must generate a question about pacing, transition, acceleration, or closure shape that survives de-binding.

## De-binding route

1. remove Noh, aesthetic, and Japanese-cultural value claims from the target-facing result;
2. translate jo / ha / kyū into provisional temporal functions;
3. locate phase boundaries only where target-side events or timing support them;
4. do not force equal duration or monotonic acceleration;
5. preserve targets with constant pace or externally fixed timing;
6. reject the framework if beginning/middle/end labels add no temporal distinction.

## Profile-ready decision

The candidate now has a historical textual witness, Noh scholarship, empirical pacing evidence, near-neighbor differentiation, de-binding, and positive/negative non-artistic examples.

It remains outside runtime. The next adoption decision should test whether the pacing operations survive across structurally different non-artistic targets without turning jo-ha-kyū into a universal three-stage recipe.
