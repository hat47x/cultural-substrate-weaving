# Theosophical sefirot candidate profile

Status: profile-ready / research-only / religious-lineage-sensitive

## Identity

- names: classical theosophical sefirot, medieval Kabbalistic sefirot, theosophical Kabbalah
- current scope: selected medieval/classical theosophical uses of ten differentiated divine powers or attributes in an emanatory relational field
- intended CSW use: differentiated mediation, relation among mediators, attribute tension, and configuration-variant probes
- not intended use: reproducing a Tree of Life, divine ontology, ritual theurgy, or later lineage-specific practice

## Source basis

### Scholarly overview

Pinchas Giller, “A Note on the Sefirot,” in *Reading the Zohar*.

https://academic.oup.com/book/1782/chapter-abstract/141450127

Useful for the historical development from the earlier term in Sefer Yetzirah into medieval theosophical Kabbalah and for the common description of sefirot as differentiated stages or aspects in divine emanation.

Useful for keeping medieval theosophical sefirot distinct from Sefer Yetzirah and for documenting historical variation in spatial/configurational representations.

### Historical-development reference

Avishai Bar-Asher and Jeremy Phillip Brown, *Light is Sown: The Cultivation of Kabbalah in Medieval Castile*, Oxford University Press, 2025.

https://academic.oup.com/book/60780/chapter-abstract/528593579

Useful for treating the development of sefirotic Kabbalah as historically emergent rather than a single timeless diagram.

## Structural core

The reusable CSW structure is not “ten levels.”

The candidate offers a field in which differentiated functions mediate between an abstract source and concrete manifestation while remaining relationally connected. Some relations can be complementary or tension-bearing rather than simply earlier/later in a pipeline.

Historical configurations varied. CSW therefore does not freeze one right/left/center diagram or one modern Tree of Life as the framework.

## Native operation candidates

### layered-mediation

Ask what differentiated mediating functions are skipped by a direct source→outcome explanation.

### emanatory-relation

Trace how a downstream manifestation changes as it passes through differentiated mediators, without treating the relation as physical causation.

### attribute-tension

Inspect whether two functions constrain, balance, or qualify one another rather than belonging to one linear rank.

### relational-ordering

Represent ordering through relation and mediation, not only through level number.

### configuration-variant-probe

Ask whether changing the arrangement of the mediators changes the interpretation, while preserving that historical diagrams themselves were contested.

## Target-return questions

- Which intermediate function is being collapsed into a direct source→outcome link?
- Are two mediators sequential, parallel, complementary, or in tension?
- Does a single vertical hierarchy erase lateral relations?
- Which ordering is target-supported and which comes only from the framework?
- If one mediator is removed, what target-side behavior actually changes?
- After removing sefirotic names and theology, does a differentiated mediation problem remain?

## Near-neighbor differentiation

- **Sāṅkhya** emphasizes production order and observer/process separation. Theosophical sefirot here emphasize differentiated mediating functions and relations among them.
- **Huayan** emphasizes whole–part reciprocity and node perspective. Theosophical sefirot retain an asymmetry between source, mediation, and manifestation rather than making every part mutually define the whole in the same way.
- **Generic hierarchy** only orders levels. This candidate is useful only when target evidence supports qualitatively different mediating functions and cross-relations.

## Historical / lineage boundary

Do not treat Zoharic, Cordoverian, Lurianic, Hasidic, Christian Kabbalistic, and Western-esoteric Tree of Life systems as one invariant structure.

This profile remains at a deliberately broad “classical theosophical” research layer. Any runtime adoption would need a narrower historical scope or a source contract showing that the chosen mediation operations genuinely survive across the cited medieval material.

## De-binding

1. remove divine names, sacred attributes, gendered symbolism, ritual efficacy, and Tree of Life iconography;
2. retain only target-supported mediator functions and their relations;
3. mark any proposed mediator as `framework_generated` until independently observed;
4. do not preserve the number ten after de-binding unless the target independently has ten relevant functions;
5. return ordering and tension claims to target-side evidence.

## Profile-ready decision

The candidate now has a bounded medieval/theosophical scope, independent scholarly sources, a distinct mediation operation, near-neighbor separation, de-binding, and positive/negative target-return examples.

It remains outside runtime because the next decision must test whether “differentiated mediation” remains distinctive enough after religious symbolism is removed, rather than collapsing into generic architecture modeling.
