# Buddhist mandala spatial candidate profile

Status: defer-lineage-specific / research-only

## Identity

- names: Buddhist mandala / maṇḍala spatial systems
- current scope: structural features documented in Himalayan and Japanese Esoteric Buddhist examples
- intended CSW use: center-periphery, cardinal differentiation, nested enclosure, gates, and approach-to-center
- not intended use: one universal "mandala psychology" or a generic decorative diagram

## Source basis

### Museum reference

Rubin Museum of Himalayan Art, Mandala

https://rubinmuseum.org/highlights/mandala/

Useful for the center, square/circle organization, cardinal quadrants, ritual/visualization function, and the fact that mandalas are embedded in specific Buddhist tantric practices.

### Museum reference

Rubin Museum of Himalayan Art, The Mandala: A Guide to Transformation

https://rubinmuseum.org/the-mandala-a-guide-to-transformation/

Useful for the relation between outer approach and center in initiation/meditation contexts.

### Museum object study

The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Mandala of the Bodhisattva Monju (Manjushri) of the Eight Syllables

https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/40137

Useful as a concrete example with a central figure, surrounding sacred sector, protective outer band, and deities associated with cardinal and ordinal directions.

## Structural core available for research

Across the present sources, several recurring spatial operations are visible:

- a distinguished center;
- differentiated cardinal or ordinal directions;
- nested or concentric sectors;
- boundary and enclosure;
- gate / access structure in many mandala types;
- movement or visualization from outside toward an inner center.

These are not claimed as one invariant template.

## Candidate operations

### center-periphery-reframe

Ask what changes if the target is organized around a center and differentiated periphery.

### directional-role-map

Ask whether different directions or sectors have different functions rather than being interchangeable locations.

### nested-boundary-pass

Represent an issue as multiple enclosures or levels of access.

### gate-threshold-probe

Ask where transition into an inner layer is constrained, mediated, or prepared.

### center-approach-sequence

Ask whether understanding or participation changes as one moves from outside to inside.

## What not to import by default

- deities, enlightened qualities, initiation authority, or tantric efficacy as target-side facts;
- one Tibetan, Indian, Chinese, or Japanese mandala as the universal pattern;
- Hindu yantra, Buddhist mandala, and Jain mandala as interchangeable;
- modern Jungian mandala psychology as the historical meaning of Buddhist mandalas;
- iconographic colors and correspondences without a lineage-specific source;
- the assumption that "center" means organizational importance in every target.

## Why status remains defer-lineage-specific

The operation space is valuable, but the source material also shows substantial variation by deity, ritual, school, region, and purpose.

Before profile-ready status, the project should choose at least one bounded corpus or lineage and document:

- which spatial features are native there;
- which are common but not invariant;
- which gates, directions, colors, or layers belong only to specific examples;
- which operations survive de-binding without pretending that all mandalas share them.

## Exploratory prompts

- Is there a meaningful center, or are we imposing one?
- Which outer elements are structurally peripheral rather than merely less important?
- Are there multiple boundaries with different access conditions?
- Which transitions require a gate or preparation step?
- Does reading from the center outward hide something that appears when reading from the boundary inward?
- Which directional distinctions survive after religious symbolism is removed?

## De-binding

1. remove deity, palace, initiation, and sacred-cosmos vocabulary unless provenance requires it;
2. retain only center, direction, layer, boundary, gate, and approach relations supported by the chosen lineage;
3. test whether the target has independent spatial or access structure;
4. keep "no real center" as a valid residual;
5. preserve lineage identity in provenance even after symbolic vocabulary is removed.

## Adoption gap

Do not adopt into runtime yet.

Next research should select a specific mandala corpus and produce a lineage-separated structure map before any general CSW dossier is created.
