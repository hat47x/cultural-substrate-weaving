# Sefer Yetzirah dimensions candidate profile

Status: profile-ready / research-only / text-version-sensitive

## Identity

- names: Sefer Yetzirah sefirot, sefirot belimah, セーフェル・イェツィラーのsefirot
- current scope: the use of ten sefirot in Sefer Yetzirah as dimensional or directional structure, kept separate from later theosophical Kabbalah
- intended CSW use: axis separation, opposed extents, multi-domain reframing, and finite-frame/open-depth tension
- not intended use: importing later Tree of Life attributes or presenting one recension as the timeless canonical text

## Source basis

### Textual access point

Sefaria Library, Sefer Yetzirah

https://www.sefaria.org/texts/Kabbalah/Sefer%20Yetzirah

Useful as a public textual access point with multiple commentary/version contexts. The Gra version is a later recension and is not treated as the historical base text for the whole tradition.

### Text-critical reference

A. Peter Hayman, *Sefer Yesira: Edition, Translation and Text-Critical Commentary*, Mohr Siebeck, 2004.

Bibliographic record:
https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb39287777w

Hayman's edition is important because Sefer Yetzirah survives in multiple recensions. Short, Long, and Saadyan textual traditions must not be collapsed into one fixed sequence.

### Scholarly structural comparison

J. H. Chajes, “Spheres, Sefirot, and the Imaginal Astronomical Discourse of Classical Kabbalah,” *Harvard Theological Review*.

https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/spheres-sefirot-and-the-imaginal-astronomical-discourse-of-classical-kabbalah/6406AC47AA34ACAFCA9E9F99823AF630

Chajes explicitly distinguishes Sefer Yetzirah's sefirot from later theosophical sefirot. In one important formulation, the former organize extents of space, time, and value rather than a medieval emanatory Godhead.

## Structural core

The useful CSW structure is **not a ten-level hierarchy**.

The candidate asks whether a target can be reframed through several independent axes or extents, including paired oppositions, rather than through one ranked scale.

The exact list of ten must remain recension-sensitive. Public versions do not all present one identical list. The reusable operation is therefore the distinction among axes, opposed extents, and finite framing—not a universal assignment of ten target categories.

## Native operation candidates

### dimension-pairing

Ask whether two apparently separate target labels are better understood as opposite extents of one axis.

### opposed-extents

Externalize both directions of an axis instead of treating one side as the default and the other as a deviation.

### axis-reframing

Replace one mixed classification with several independent axes when the target evidence supports that separation.

### finite-count-open-depth

Hold a bounded set of axes or positions without assuming the internal variation along each axis is exhausted by the named endpoints.

## Target-return questions

- Are two labels actually opposite ends of one dimension?
- Which current distinctions are independent axes rather than levels of one hierarchy?
- Is one endpoint being treated as normal and the other as exceptional?
- Does separating temporal, spatial, evaluative, or other target-owned axes remove a false contradiction?
- Which axis is framework-generated rather than independently present in the target?
- If the Sefer Yetzirah vocabulary is removed, do the independent-axis questions still survive?

## Near-neighbor differentiation

- **Tibetan Buddhist mandala** contributes center/periphery, directions, gates, and nested access. Sefer Yetzirah here contributes opposed extents and axis separation without requiring a privileged center.
- **Sāṅkhya** contributes production layers and an observer/process split. Sefer Yetzirah dimensions do not imply a production sequence.
- **Theosophical sefirot** contribute differentiated mediation in an emanatory relational field. The shared name and number do not license structural merger.

## Text-version boundary

Do not treat the Gra recension, Short recension, Long recension, and Saadyan recension as interchangeable wordings of one fixed diagram.

A target operation may be sourced from a documented formulation, but CSW must preserve which textual layer supports it. If a proposed operation depends on a feature that disappears in another major recension, that dependence stays explicit.

## De-binding

1. remove Hebrew names, cosmology, and creation claims from the target-facing result;
2. retain only target-supported axes, extents, and opposition relations;
3. label any newly proposed axis as `framework_generated`;
4. do not force the target into ten positions;
5. return each axis to target-side observations or definitions before attribution.

## Profile-ready decision

The candidate now has a text-critical boundary, a scholarly distinction from later sefirot, native operations, near-neighbor separation, de-binding, and positive/negative target-return examples.

It remains outside runtime until the team decides whether axis-reframing adds enough beyond generic dimensional analysis to justify carrying a religious-text-derived dossier in the adopted corpus.
