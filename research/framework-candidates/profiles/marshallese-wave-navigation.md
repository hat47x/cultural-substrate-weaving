# Marshallese wave navigation / stick-chart candidate profile

Status: profile-ready / research-only / living-tradition-sensitive

## Identity

- names: Marshallese wave navigation, Marshallese stick charts, wave piloting
- current scope: publicly documented relations among ocean swell patterns, islands/atolls, route memory, instructional models, and embodied wayfinding
- intended CSW use: route-oriented network probing, relative-position reasoning, model-to-environment return, cue-sequence inspection, and distinction between instructional representation and situated sensing
- not intended use: reconstructing restricted navigator knowledge, issuing navigation advice, or claiming access to family/school-specific expertise

## Source basis

### Smithsonian object record — navigation chart

https://www.si.edu/object/nmnhanthropology_8552873

Institutional collection record describing an abstract illustration of how ocean swells interact with land. The record explicitly notes that the chart is an instructional tool used before a voyage rather than a real-time navigation instrument.

### Smithsonian Ocean — Micronesian stick charts

https://ocean.si.edu/human-connections/history-cultures/navigating-waters-micronesian-stick-charts

Institutional educational overview distinguishing chart types used as models or piloting instructions, emphasizing that charts were memorized on land and that navigation at sea relied on embodied wave sensing rather than literal chart reading.

### Joseph H. Genz — Resolving Ambivalence in Marshallese Navigation

https://escholarship.org/uc/item/43h1d0d7

Scholarly study of Marshallese wave navigation and the reinterpretation/revival of stick-chart wave models. Important for the caution that family-based knowledge, different navigation schools, historical disruption, and ambiguous external interpretation make the tradition unsafe to flatten into one invariant diagram grammar.

### Raigetal / Kelen / Genz et al. — seafaring systems

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11457-025-09456-4

Recent practitioner-linked scholarly record including Marshallese and Yapese navigators/builders. Useful for keeping the charts connected to lived seafaring, memorization, swell navigation, and transmission rather than treating museum objects as self-sufficient maps.

## Structural core

The useful CSW structure is not “a strange map.” It is a relation among at least four different things that must not be collapsed:

1. **relative environmental structure**
   - islands/atolls, swell patterns, current/wave interactions, and route cues are represented relationally rather than as a metrically exact coordinate map;
2. **instructional model**
   - a chart can externalize selected relations for teaching, rehearsal, or memory;
3. **memorized route / cue structure**
   - the representation is learned before travel and does not need to be consulted as a literal live map;
4. **situated embodied sensing**
   - actual wayfinding depends on perceiving the sea and continuously returning abstract knowledge to experienced environmental cues.

Public sources describe multiple chart forms and terminologies, but their typologies and interpretations are historically variable. CSW should therefore preserve the operation-level distinction above rather than freeze one universal chart grammar.

## Native operation candidates

### relative-route model

Represent a target as nodes plus route-relevant relational cues without assuming a global coordinate system.

Questions:

- Which locations matter only relative to another location?
- Which relation is directional without being metric?
- Which path is remembered through transitions among cues rather than through absolute position?

### model / environment return

Separate a preparatory model from the live situation.

Questions:

- What can be learned or rehearsed in the model?
- What must be re-sensed in the actual environment?
- Which part of the model becomes unreliable when conditions differ?

### cue-sequence wayfinding

Trace a route through ordered or conditional cues rather than a single destination coordinate.

Questions:

- What cue should appear before the next decision?
- Which missing cue means the route model should be reopened?
- Is the route recoverable from local cues even when global position is uncertain?

### disturbance-as-signal

Ask whether a perturbation carries location information rather than being treated only as noise.

Questions:

- Does interaction around an obstacle create a recognizable pattern?
- Which disturbance is diagnostic and which is merely incidental?
- Can the target be inferred from changes in surrounding flow rather than direct observation?

### embodied / abstract complementarity

Keep abstract representation and situated sensing as different but cooperating modes.

Questions:

- Which claim exists only in the diagram?
- Which knowledge is available only through situated experience?
- Where do the two disagree, and what target-side observation can adjudicate?

## Target-return questions

- Are we treating a route as a sequence of local cues when the target actually requires a global coordinate, or vice versa?
- Which relation in the target is relative, directional, or conditional rather than metric?
- What part of the current model is only a rehearsal aid and must be checked in the live environment?
- Which environmental perturbations may contain information rather than merely adding noise?
- Can a local cue update the path without rebuilding the whole map?
- What is being memorized: a literal picture, a relation among cues, or a procedure for sensing change?
- If the navigation vocabulary is removed, do relative-route, cue-sequence, model/environment return, and embodied-check operations still survive?

## Boundaries

- Do not treat stick charts as literal modern maps or assume distances are to scale.
- Do not claim one fixed chart typology or notation applies to all Marshallese navigators, periods, families, or schools.
- Do not infer restricted or family-held navigational knowledge from public museum records.
- Do not present CSW as reproducing Marshallese navigator expertise.
- Do not convert traditional ocean knowledge into modern physical/oceanographic fact claims without independent evidence.
- Do not use this profile for real-world marine navigation or safety decisions.
- Do not reduce embodied wave sensing to a generic “intuition” metaphor.
- Do not turn relative-route structure into evidence that the target has no global coordinate system.
- Keep colonial collection histories and historical disruption in view when interpreting museum artifacts.

## De-binding route

1. remove island, swell, shell, and canoe terminology from the target-side representation;
2. retain only the distinction among node, route cue, relative relation, instructional model, and situated observation;
3. mark which target relations are metric, merely directional, sequential, or conditional;
4. model a possible route as local cue transitions rather than assuming a complete global map;
5. return each route/cue claim to target-side observations, logs, actions, or environmental feedback;
6. keep uncertainty when the model and situated evidence disagree;
7. preserve the distinction between a representation used for rehearsal and evidence obtained during actual execution.

## Near-neighbor differentiation

### vs Jingluo

Both can open route/network questions.

Marshallese wave navigation differs by emphasizing:

- non-medical wayfinding;
- relative environmental cues;
- preparatory models vs situated sensing;
- route updates through perturbation and embodied observation.

Jingluo is valuable specifically because it preserves main/collateral channel organization inside a traditional medical system. These should not be collapsed into one generic network metaphor.

### vs generic graph / map

A generic graph already represents nodes and edges.

The additional operation here is the distinction between:

- a non-metric or selectively abstract route representation;
- what is memorized before traversal;
- what must be sensed during traversal;
- disturbances that may themselves become navigational cues.

### vs affinity spatial layout

Affinity layout records analytic geometry chosen by an analyst.

Marshallese wave-navigation structure instead contributes **wayfinding through environmental relations and cue sequences**. Layout alone does not reproduce this operation.
