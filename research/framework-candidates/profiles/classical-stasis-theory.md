# Classical stasis theory candidate profile

Status: runtime requalification / adopted candidate / restored-discovery-aware

## Identity

- names: stasis theory / status theory / στάσις / constitutio
- current scope: issue classification in Greco-Roman rhetorical theory, especially Hermagorean/Ciceronian/Quintilian traditions
- intended CSW use: determine what kind of disagreement is actually active before choosing an argumentative operation
- not intended use: one timeless four-box taxonomy or a truth test

## Source basis

### Primary text

Cicero, *De Inventione*, book 1, especially the discussion of constitutio / issue types and the translative issue.

https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A2008.01.0683%3Abook%3D1%3Asection%3D16

Cicero distinguishes kinds of controversy and treats a translative issue as a dispute about who, with whom, how, before whom, by what right, or at what time a case ought to be conducted.

### Primary / classical rhetorical synthesis

Quintilian, *Institutio Oratoria*, book 3, chapters 5–6.

https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A2007.01.0060%3Abook%3D3%3Achapter%3D5

Quintilian reviews competing ancient stasis schemes and explicitly notes variation among systems. His discussion links conjecture with fact, definition with naming/category, quality with evaluation, and legal/translative questions with competence or procedure.

### Scholarly reconstruction

Malcolm Heath, “The Substructure of stasis-theory from Hermagoras to Hermogenes,” *The Classical Quarterly* 44.1 (1994), 114–129.

https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/abs/substructure-of-stasistheory-from-hermagoras-to-hermogenes/3367411C59C61208776EA1E503C2D8D7

Heath describes stasis-theory as a way to classify rhetorical problems by the underlying structure of the dispute and shows that the detailed schemes vary historically.

## Structural core

The useful CSW structure is **issue-state discrimination** rather than “four questions” as a form.

A working cross-source minimum can distinguish:

1. **fact / conjecture** — whether the alleged event, condition, or claim is the case;
2. **definition** — what category, name, or description properly applies;
3. **quality / evaluation** — how an accepted act or state should be evaluated, justified, excused, praised, blamed, or weighed;
4. **procedure / competence / translation** — whether this forum, actor, timing, rule, or mode is the proper one for deciding the dispute.

The historical theories do not all package these in exactly the same way. CSW should therefore preserve the operation while keeping variant boundaries explicit.

## Candidate operations

### dispute-state-separation

Split apparently unified disagreement into fact, definition, quality, and procedure/competence layers.

### fact-before-evaluation probe

Ask whether participants are evaluating an event whose occurrence or description is still disputed.

### definition-shift audit

Detect when disagreement has moved from “did it happen?” to “what counts as this kind of thing?”

### quality-after-concession probe

When the basic event is conceded, ask what normative/evaluative question remains.

### competence-procedure probe

Ask who may decide, in which forum, by which rule, at what time, and whether procedural disagreement is distinct from the substantive dispute.

### stasis-switch detection

Track when interlocutors answer different issue types and therefore appear to refute one another while actually changing the question.

## Useful outputs

- issue-state card;
- unresolved fact question;
- competing definitions;
- evaluation criteria question;
- forum/authority/process question;
- stasis-switch residual;
- “same evidence, different issue” contrast.

## Target-return questions

- Are the parties disputing whether something happened, what it should be called, how it should be judged, or who/how it should be decided?
- If the factual claim were conceded, would the disagreement remain?
- If both sides used the same definition, would the disagreement remain?
- Is an evaluative argument being used to answer a factual question?
- Is a procedural objection being mistaken for a denial of the underlying event?
- Has the argument shifted issue type without acknowledging the shift?
- Which target-side record would settle the current issue, and which records would be irrelevant because they answer another stasis?

## Distinction from nearby CSW frameworks

- **Nyāya five-member inference** externalizes the bridge from thesis and reason through example/application to conclusion. Stasis theory asks **which kind of question the argument is trying to settle**.
- **Aristotle four causes** pluralizes explanatory “why” questions. Stasis theory separates kinds of controversy or issue.
- **Catuṣkoṭi** can reopen proposition space beyond a binary frame. Stasis theory does not expand truth-value possibilities; it locates the dispute.
- **Confucian role/li** shifts perspective through relation and role. Stasis theory may ask whether a role dispute is factual, definitional, evaluative, or procedural, but does not supply the role norm.

## Historical / variant boundary

There is no single invariant “classical stasis grid.”

- Hermagoras, Cicero, the *Rhetorica ad Herennium*, Quintilian, Hermogenes, and later traditions organize issues differently.
- Cicero himself presents different schemes in different works.
- “Translative,” “legal,” “jurisdictional,” and procedural issues do not map one-to-one across every author.
- Later classroom simplifications such as “fact / definition / quality / policy” can be useful modern teaching tools, but they must not be projected backward as the one ancient canonical form.

For CSW, the cross-source minimum above is a **de-bound cognitive operation**, not a claim that the ancient authors agreed on one four-cell table.

## What not to import by default

- forensic guilt, legal authority, or Roman procedural norms as target-side facts;
- the assumption that every disagreement has exactly one stasis;
- the assumption that identifying the stasis resolves the dispute;
- the assumption that a procedural objection is merely evasive;
- the assumption that factual evidence can settle an evaluative question, or vice versa;
- a modern simplified stasis chart presented as an ancient universal taxonomy.

## De-binding route

Before returning a stasis pass to the target:

1. remove Greek/Latin labels unless provenance matters;
2. state the live question in target vocabulary;
3. separate fact, definition, evaluation, and procedure only where the target material actually supports those distinctions;
4. preserve multiple simultaneous issue states when the dispute genuinely contains them;
5. identify what evidence would answer each issue type;
6. keep historical rhetorical taxonomy as provenance, not target-side authority.

## Runtime requalification

The former runtime dossier was removed before CSW separated capability overlap from discovery value.

On the existing release-incident target, a deliberately constructed ordinary issue-triage baseline can reproduce the target-relevant de-bound operations once the issue-separation job is already known:

- issue-state separation;
- fact-before-evaluation;
- event-to-definition shift detection;
- evaluation after concession;
- authority / procedure separation;
- issue-switch detection;
- evidence relevance by issue type.

That remains valid **capability-overlap** evidence.

### Discovery-value comparison

See:

- `research/framework-candidates/comparisons/classical-stasis-discovery-value.md`

The retrospective ex-ante comparison starts from ordinary incident/postmortem practice without predefining a fact / definition / evaluation / authority table.

That baseline naturally reconstructs timeline, impact, causes, mitigation, responder decisions, roles, lessons, and corrective actions. It can contain all four disputed statements without necessarily exposing that they answer different **kinds of question**.

Stasis contact adds a distinct discovery move:

- identify the live issue type before treating a reply as a rebuttal;
- ask what disagreement remains after conceding the fact;
- ask what remains after fixing the definition;
- separate evidence relevance by issue type;
- detect a silent switch from fact to definition, evaluation, or competence/procedure.

After this job is visible, the target-language issue table from the capability comparison becomes an appropriate implementation.

### Retrospective requalification result

The revised evidence supports **runtime restoration**.

The reason is not unique formal capability. Ordinary issue triage can represent the same structure after it has been selected.

The reason is discovery contribution: on the tested disagreement target, generic ex-ante incident review does not reliably force issue-type discrimination or stasis-switch detection, while stasis contact exposes those jobs directly and the resulting questions survive de-binding.

The operational runtime restoration has now been completed. The framework is again available in the default runtime, with activation limited by the same non-activation boundary and discovery-aware rationale recorded here.

Preserve the non-activation boundary: do not activate stasis merely because a disagreement or incident exists. Activate it when the missing cognitive job is locating what kind of question is actually disputed, determining which evidence can answer it, or detecting an unacknowledged issue switch.
