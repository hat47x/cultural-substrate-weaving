# Greco-Roman method of loci vs Vedic recitation vs khipu vs Marshallese navigation

Status: research differentiation / no runtime promotion

## Purpose

All four candidates can look like "structure helps us not lose information", but they preserve different objects and use different return mechanisms.

The comparison is deliberately operation-centered. Cultural names do not make two structures different, and superficial spatial vocabulary does not make them equivalent.

## Core distinction

| Framework | Stable structure | Variable / observed material | Primary operation | Target-return question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greco-Roman method of loci | ordered distinguishable loci | images or cues bound to loci | traverse a stable index to reconstruct ordered content | where does the ordered target stop matching the scaffold? |
| Vedic recitation pathas | rule-governed alternate recitation forms | the same verbal sequence under transformed views | cross-check boundaries/order through redundant sequence forms | did normalization or transmission alter adjacency/order? |
| Inka khipu record structure | physical attachment hierarchy and positional channels | encoded values / structured but partly undeciphered channels | preserve hierarchy, position, and unexplained channels during representation | what structure was lost when the record was flattened? |
| Marshallese wave navigation | route model and learned relative relations | live environmental cues and disturbances | update wayfinding from changing cue sequences | what does the environment now indicate relative to the route? |

## Same engineering target, different contact

Target:

> A long migration procedure is repeatedly summarized, and reviewers suspect that important steps disappear.

### Method-of-loci pass

Build or reuse a stable checkpoint sequence and bind the current procedure to it. Traverse the sequence and localize missing or swapped positions.

Potential yield:

> "restore rehearsal is absent at L4; post-rollback verification is absent at L12."

### Vedic-recitation-inspired pass

Represent the same ordered procedure through alternate local sequence views, emphasizing adjacency and boundary fidelity rather than a separate spatial index.

Potential yield:

> "step B→C exists in the compact form, but the transformed adjacency view shows that C→D was lost during normalization."

### Khipu-inspired pass

Ask whether flattening the procedure into prose discarded hierarchy, attachment, grouping, or a parallel channel.

Potential yield:

> "rollback criteria were a separate child structure in the source representation but were flattened into a note under cutover."

### Marshallese-navigation-inspired pass

This candidate is weak unless execution supplies changing live cues. During an actual migration, it could instead ask how observed system signals update the route.

Potential yield:

> "replication lag changed the next safe action; the static plan must be updated from the live environment."

## Ordinary-checklist baseline

A numbered checklist is the strongest challenge to method-of-loci distinctness.

If the only surviving de-bound operations are:

- assign a number;
- walk numbers in order;
- notice a blank;

then the cultural framework has contributed no clear additional generator.

Possible residual distinctness would need to come from the explicit separation between a reusable stable scaffold and replaceable bindings, or from the way a learned/visualized traversal exposes position-specific reconstruction failures. That must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

## Non-substitutability boundary

- Method of loci is not a substitute for redundant sequence transformations.
- Vedic pathas are not an arbitrary storage index.
- Khipu is a record structure, not merely a memory cue system.
- Marshallese navigation depends on environment return and should not be frozen into static loci.
- None of them should replace the target source of truth.

## Runtime decision

No runtime promotion.

The direct same-target comparison against a plain numbered checklist is now recorded in `method-of-loci-vs-numbered-checklist.md`. On the migration-review target, the checklist reproduces the target-relevant de-bound operations and the same useful questions with lower representational overhead.

Method of loci therefore does not currently fill a general CSW runtime gap. Keep it as a sourced research reference and reconsider only for a target where human memory, rehearsal, or spatial mnemonic binding is itself materially relevant.
