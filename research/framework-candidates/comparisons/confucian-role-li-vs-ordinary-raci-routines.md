# Confucian role / li versus RACI, decision logs, and organizational routine analysis

Status: runtime requalification / matched capability baseline

## Test question

Once the target-side work is named, can ordinary methods reproduce it without importing Confucian normative authority?

Do not confuse this post-selection capability comparison with a generic method being selected *before* framework contact.

## Fixed hypothetical targets

**A — title/authority dispute:** After a release incident, "the owner failed to decide." The designated product owner lacks the production-release approval permission; the platform release lead performs the authorization. The project maintains a RACI sheet and an approval log.

**B — patterned interaction:** The same change is reviewed in a weekly release meeting. A facilitator gives the lead engineer the first and last turn. Other engineers habitually interpret the final "any objections?" question after that lead's endorsement as administrative closure rather than a genuine invitation to dissent. A revised meeting design explicitly solicits independent objections before the lead recommendation. The interpretation and effect are **hypotheses**, not observed facts, pending recordings/interviews and before/after comparison.

## Matched capability methods

**RACI / responsibility assignment:** PMI's project lexicon identifies RACI as a responsibility-assignment matrix clarifying who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed. Approval records and RBAC/authorization logs can check whether the named owner has and exercises the relevant authority.

- PMI Lexicon (RACI): https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/registered/pdf/pmbok-standards/pmi-lexicon-pm-terms.pdf
- PMI on assigning roles: https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/delegate-project-tasks-team-responsibilities-2194

**Organizational routine studies:** Feldman and Pentland (2003) distinguish the abstract/ostensive form of a routine from the particular people, actions, places, and times through which it is performed. They describe a reciprocal relation in which performances can reproduce and change the routine. This is a strong ordinary non-cultural technique for observing meeting patterns and their possible consequences.

- Feldman & Pentland (2003), *Reconceptualizing Organizational Routines as a Source of Flexibility and Change*: https://doi.org/10.2307/3556620
- Feldman (2000), *Organizational Routines as a Source of Continuous Change*: https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.11.6.611.12529

The target B review could compare who was consulted, timing of requests, who spoke before a leader's endorsement, how silence was interpreted, and actual objection rates. These questions can be expressed without Confucian vocabulary once selected.

## Historical and epistemic boundary

No equivalence of `li` with RACI or corporate meetings is claimed. In early Confucian sources, `li` concerns cultivated conduct, respect, relationship, and ritual in historically specific moral-political orders. CSW extracts only provisional questions, never historical hierarchy as normative authority or ritual psychology as proven causation.

**Result:** matched capability overlap is strong, especially for target A; target B is also expressible through organizational routines once that method is deliberately selected. Capability overlap alone does not decide default runtime adoption.
