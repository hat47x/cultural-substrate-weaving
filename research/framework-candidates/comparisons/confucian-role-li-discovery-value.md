# Confucian role / li — ex-ante discovery-value comparison

Status: bounded qualitative desk comparison / not an experiment

## Question

Is the target-side role/ritual job selected by ordinary **generic** project/incident review before Confucian contact, or only by a specialist routine-analysis method chosen after seeing the framework output?

Use the same hypothetical material as the matched capability comparison. Do not claim that asking a question proves its answer or that a Confucian concept is true of the team.

## Fixed target A — "the owner failed to decide"

Facts supplied: a named product owner, a release lead who exercised approval authority, role and permission records, and an apparent accountability mismatch.

### Ordinary ex-ante project/incident review

Reviewers naturally ask who was responsible/accountable, who could approve, and whether an escalation was recorded. RACI plus authorization logs make role-name versus enacted authority visible **without** Confucian contact. Role-perspective switching and an obligation/permission map follow readily.

### Confucian contact

The framework asks which relation carries the expectation, whether the title is actually enacted, and which role-position changes interpretation.

**Residual:** no clear new target-side job for this fixed target. If all CSW adopted use were like A, demotion would be justified.

## Fixed target B — a repeatedly staged release meeting

Hypothetical facts: the same release meeting uses a fixed sequence in which the lead gives a recommendation before the chair's last "objections?" prompt. The incident records show that the formal consultation rule was satisfied, yet people did not raise objections in that meeting; some objections appeared later in one-to-one review. Whether the ritual caused silence is **unknown**.

### Generic ex-ante project/incident baseline

The normal review asks:

- who was accountable for the release decision?;
- was the consultation/approval step completed?;
- did the runbook or RACI omit a decision maker?;
- which evidence and corrective actions are needed?;
- whether communication and escalation worked.

Such an audit may conclude that the formal consultation occurred and recommend clearer roles or more time to comment. It does **not require** treating a patterned **sequence of enactment** as changing the social meaning of an otherwise identical "any objections?" utterance.

### Confucian contact

The de-bound `li` / patterned-conduct pass asks:

- Did "any objections?" function as an invitation **before** the lead recommendation but as closure **after** it?
- Which position in the relation makes public disagreement costly, expected, or respectful?
- Which repeated practice may be training the team's expectation of when a decision is already settled?
- Does changing the order or opening form change what can be said, holding decision authority constant?
- What evidence would refute the ritual-context hypothesis?

These are hypotheses about the **enacted interaction context**, not moral judgments about participants. The critical discovery is not "be courteous" or "respect leaders"; it is that **the same formally authorized speech act may mean something different within a repeated relational pattern**.

### Post-contact specialist baseline

Organizational-routine research (ostensive / performative, specific people and time/place) is fully capable of expressing this inquiry. But selecting that specialist lens *after* the role/li contact does not establish that a routine-analysis investigation would have been commissioned by the generic incident reviewer ex ante.

## Ex-ante comparison

| Job | Generic pre-contact project review | Confucian role/li contact | Qualitative incremental discovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| clarify named owner and actual authority | naturally selected | name-behavior congruence | low |
| map consultation and reciprocal expectations | naturally selected | relation/obligation map | low |
| ask whether the meeting sequence changes meaning of the same words | not structurally required | ritual-context-shift | plausible |
| ask whether repeated enactment trains deference/attention over time | not a default incident question | practice-shapes-disposition probe | plausible **hypothesis only** |
| audit actual participant meaning and falsifiers | possible once routine-analysis is selected | target-return probes | requires target evidence |

## Counter-hypothesis and limits

The strongest counter-hypothesis is that competent facilitation, human-factors or organizational-routine review would choose the exact same pattern questions without Confucian contact. In organizations where meeting design/psychological safety is already the focal problem, this is likely. That is the **non-activation** boundary.

The current record is a conceptual pre-registration, not an empirical proof that ordinary reviewers miss the question or that the framework increases real decisions. A blinded before/after comparison may reverse this decision. Do not generalize B to all disagreements or assume ritual causes silence from temporal association.

## De-binding and target return

Remove `li`, virtue, historical hierarchy, and cultural moral language. Return to:

1. **Observed structure:** order of utterances; participation records; scope and formal authority.
2. **Generated question:** does repeated ordering change the recognized permission to object?
3. **Required evidence:** recordings, participant accounts with anonymity controls, same-team before/after or matched meetings, alternate explanations (fear of retaliation, deadline, decision already final).
4. **Possible action:** trial of independent objections before lead endorsement, *only if* target evidence supports the hypothesis; record reversible evaluation.
5. **No-framework:** valid if the generic review or existing human-factors routine review already asks these questions.

## Decision

**Bounded runtime retention is supported** for target B, because a distinct *discovery question* about enacted relational pattern appears before ordinary generic decision/accountability review would necessarily select a specialist routine method. It is not unique analytic capability; the matched routine method can express it.

Retain the runtime candidate with a narrow selection cue: repeated meeting/interaction form, identical speech with changed contextual meaning, role-conditioned silence/permission, or practice-shaped expectations. Do **not** trigger merely because an organizational title, stakeholder, disagreement, or RACI exists.

Near-neighbor boundary: Huayan explores part/whole identity under a different containing context, whereas role/li concerns actor positions and **enacted relational conduct**. Their structural difference is real but is not the sole evidence for retention.
