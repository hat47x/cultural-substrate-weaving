# Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics discovery-value comparison

Status: retrospective runtime requalification / discovery-value comparison

## Purpose

The prior demotion established that a strong policy / requirements review can reproduce the de-bound Mīmāṃsā operations after those interpretive jobs are named.

That is **capability-overlap** evidence.

This comparison asks the missing question:

> before Mīmāṃsā contact, would ordinary policy / requirements review naturally select prescription segmentation, attachment/scope review, explicit-vs-inferred marking, and conflict decomposition?

## Fixed targets

Use the two existing SIer fixtures.

### Deployment policy

> For production changes, obtain approval. Emergency changes may proceed immediately when service restoration cannot wait. Record the reason afterward.

### Runbook

> When cached data is stale, refresh it. If the refresh service is unavailable, stale data may be served for up to ten minutes. After recovery, refresh immediately.

## Ex-ante generic policy / requirements baseline

Do not name Mīmāṃsā, Sanskrit linguistic categories, or a specialist hermeneutic framework.

Review the text as operational requirements / policy.

Ask:

- How many independent requirements or permissions are present?
- Who is the responsible actor?
- What action is required, permitted, or prohibited?
- What condition activates the rule?
- What exception changes the rule?
- What phrase modifies which requirement?
- What duration or temporal boundary applies?
- What follow-up action is required?
- Which linkage is written explicitly, and which is an assumption or interpretation?
- Are any statements ambiguous or open to multiple readings?
- Are any requirements inconsistent or conflicting?
- Which ambiguity must be returned to the policy owner rather than silently resolved?

These questions arise directly from ordinary requirements quality work.

## Domain-standard selection path

NASA's current requirements guidance makes this ex-ante path explicit.

The Systems Engineering Handbook's requirement-writing checklist asks for:

- one thought per requirement statement;
- one subject and one predicate;
- clear and unambiguous wording;
- assumptions to be explicitly stated;
- consistency without contradictions;
- traceability and correct requirement level.

NASA Software Engineering Handbook guidance likewise requires requirements to be complete, correct, consistent, independent, unambiguous, understandable, traceable, and reviewed for ambiguous, conflicting, or incomplete content.

Sources:

- NASA Systems Engineering Handbook, Appendix C — How to Write a Good Requirement:
  https://www.nasa.gov/reference/appendix-c-how-to-write-a-good-requirement/
- NASA Software Engineering Handbook, SWE-049 Document Software Requirements:
  https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16449789/SWE-049%2B-%2BDocument%2BSoftware%2BRequirements
- NASA Software Engineering Handbook, SWE-051 Software Requirements Analysis:
  https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16450593/SWE-051%2B-%2BSoftware%2BRequirements%2BAnalysis

This does not make modern requirements engineering historically equivalent to Mīmāṃsā.

It shows that, for policy/runbook targets, the target category itself selects segmentation, ambiguity, scope, assumptions, and consistency work before Mīmāṃsā contact.

## Generic baseline on the deployment policy

The review naturally separates:

1. production change -> approval required;
2. emergency + restoration cannot wait -> immediate change permitted;
3. afterward -> reason-recording obligation.

It then asks:

- does "afterward" attach only to emergency change or to every production change?;
- does the exception suspend approval, delay it, or create a separate path?;
- who records the reason?;
- is the actor explicit?;
- do approval and immediate action conflict under the same condition?;
- which interpretation is explicit, and which is inferred?

## Generic baseline on the runbook

The review naturally separates:

1. stale cache -> refresh;
2. refresh service unavailable -> stale data permitted for <=10 minutes;
3. recovery -> refresh immediately.

It then asks:

- what does "for up to ten minutes" modify?;
- does recovery terminate the exception?;
- is the second refresh the same operational action?;
- can "serve stale" and "refresh immediately" apply simultaneously?;
- which linkage is textually explicit versus supplied by system context?

These are the same target-side questions that survived the Mīmāṃsā pass.

## Mīmāṃsā contact

The de-bound pass asks:

- is this one prescription or several?;
- which words/clauses expect one another syntactically?;
- which connections are semantically fit?;
- what is explicit and what is context-supplied?;
- do apparent norms really conflict under the same agent, action, time, condition, and purpose?;
- does purpose/action unity justify grouping adjacent clauses?

These remain coherent and useful questions.

## Ex-ante comparison

| Cognitive job | Generic / ex-ante policy review | Mīmāṃsā contact | Incremental discovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| split compound normative text | one thought / one requirement / one subject-predicate | prescriptive-unit detection | low |
| inspect modifier / exception scope | ordinary grammar + ambiguity review | syntactic expectancy / proximity | low |
| reject semantically strained reading | requirements correctness / domain consistency | semantic fitness | low |
| distinguish written vs supplied | assumptions explicit; derived/inferred material marked | explicit-vs-context-supplied | low |
| compare overlapping rules | consistency / conflict review | norm-conflict decomposition | low |
| inspect actor/action/condition/time | requirement normalization and operational conditions | conflict dimensions | low |
| preserve Bhāṭṭa / Prābhākara alternatives | absent | historical theory | historical/comparative residue |

## Target-return residual

After removing Mīmāṃsā vocabulary, the useful questions are:

- Where does one actionable requirement begin and end?
- What does this modifier or exception attach to?
- Which interpretation is grammatically and operationally coherent?
- What was stated explicitly?
- What assumption or contextual supplementation was introduced?
- Do two requirements actually apply to the same actor, action, time, condition, and purpose?
- Which ambiguity must be returned to the owner?

These are useful, but on the tested policy/runbook targets they are already opened by ordinary ex-ante requirements review.

## Discovery-value result

The discovery-value counter-hypothesis survives.

Unlike classical stasis theory, where a generic incident review does not necessarily ask whether participants are answering different kinds of questions, ambiguous natural-language requirements already trigger segmentation, clarity, scope, assumption, and consistency review in ordinary engineering practice.

Mīmāṃsā contributes a historically rich lineage and distinctive theories of sentence meaning. Those remain valuable research assets.

But after de-binding on these tested SIer targets, the framework does not expose a target-side interpretive job that ordinary policy / requirements review would be unlikely to select at comparable cost.

## Runtime consequence

The existing operational demotion is **confirmed under the discovery-aware standard**.

Combined evidence now shows:

- matched policy / requirements review reproduces the capability;
- generic ex-ante requirements practice naturally selects segmentation, attachment/scope, explicit/inferred, and conflict work;
- no additional target-side discovery survives on either tested fixture;
- near-neighbor distinctness from stasis and Nyāya does not create runtime value by itself.

Preserve Mīmāṃsā as a sourced research candidate for comparative hermeneutics, explicit Mīmāṃsā requests, historical / philosophical targets, education, and future targets where a distinct discovery contribution can be demonstrated.
